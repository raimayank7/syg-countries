Nominal vs real prices

The history tables show the price as recorded in each year: a nominal price. Because the general level of prices rises over time, a nominal price that goes up 30% over ten years may have fallen or stayed flat compared with other things you buy. A real price removes general inflation by using a price index.

This site does not make that adjustment, because the right index differs by country and by purpose, and an adjustment made here would look more exact than it is. If you want a real comparison, divide each year's price by your country's consumer price index for that year (relative to a base year).

Also check the unit: US prices on this site are cents per kWh, Europe's are euro per kWh (100 cents make a dollar or a euro, so 15 cents is 0.15 of a dollar or euro).
