What is a kilowatt-hour?

A kilowatt-hour (kWh) is the unit on an electricity bill: the energy used by something that draws 1,000 watts (1 kilowatt) for one hour. A 100-watt television uses 0.1 kWh each hour; ten hours of it uses 1 kWh.

The sum

kWh = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours. Cost = kWh × the price per kWh. Example at 15 cents per kWh: a 1,500 W heater for 4 hours uses 1.5 × 4 = 6 kWh and costs 6 × 15 = 90 cents.

Where to find the watts

Look on the label or the power adapter of the appliance. Heating appliances (heaters, kettles, irons) use the most; motors and electronics use less. Many appliances do not run at full power all the time, so the label gives the maximum.
