Countries of the Americas

The Americas are the lands of the western half of the world: North America, Central America, the Caribbean islands and South America. They run almost from pole to pole, from the Arctic north of Canada to Cape Horn at the tip of South America.

The region in numbers

This region has 56 entries on this site, of which 35 are member states of the United Nations. Together they hold about 1.05 billion people, roughly 13 percent of the world total, on about 42.3 million square kilometres of land. United States has the most people (about 342 million). Canada is the largest by area (9,984,670 km²), and Saint Kitts and Nevis is the smallest member state (261 km²). 2 of its member states are landlocked: Bolivia, Paraguay.

Rich and poor, crowded and empty

By GDP per person among member states, the highest figures in the data are United States ($90,000), Canada ($55,700), Bahamas ($39,500), and the lowest are Haiti ($2,700), Nicaragua ($3,200), Venezuela ($3,500). A baby can expect the longest life in Canada (82.1 years) and the shortest in Haiti (65.1 years), a gap of 17 years inside one region. The most crowded member state is Barbados, with about 656 people to each square kilometre, and the emptiest is Guyana, with about 3.9. Figures are World Bank values for the year shown on each country page.

Borders, languages, money and time

Brazil has the most land neighbours in the region, 10 of them. 11 member states have no land border in the data, being islands or groups of islands: Antigua and Barbuda, Bahamas, Barbados, Cuba, Dominica, Grenada, Jamaica, Saint Kitts and Nevis, Saint Lucia, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, Trinidad and Tobago. The languages listed for the most places here are English (34), Spanish (24), French (13), Dutch (5). The most widely shared money is the United States dollar, the main currency of 9 entries. Clocks in the region run from UTC-11:00 to UTC+12:00, a spread of 23 hours.
