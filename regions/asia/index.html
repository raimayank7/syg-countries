Countries of Asia

Asia is the largest continent and home to more than half of the people in the world. It reaches from Turkey and the Arabian Peninsula in the west to Japan in the east, and from Siberia in the north to the islands of Indonesia in the south.

The region in numbers

This region has 50 entries on this site, of which 46 are member states of the United Nations. Together they hold about 4.82 billion people, roughly 59 percent of the world total, on about 31.7 million square kilometres of land. India has the most people (about 1.46 billion). China is the largest by area (9,596,960 km²), and Maldives is the smallest member state (300 km²). 12 of its member states are landlocked: Afghanistan, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Bhutan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Laos, Mongolia, Nepal, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan.

Rich and poor, crowded and empty

By GDP per person among member states, the highest figures in the data are Singapore ($98,800), Qatar ($72,500), Israel ($60,300), and the lowest are Afghanistan ($420), Yemen ($630), Syria ($1,100). A baby can expect the longest life in Kuwait (84.6 years) and the shortest in Afghanistan (66.3 years), a gap of 18 years inside one region. The most crowded member state is Singapore, with about 8,303 people to each square kilometre, and the emptiest is Mongolia, with about 2.3. Figures are World Bank values for the year shown on each country page.

Borders, languages, money and time

China has the most land neighbours in the region, 16 of them. 5 member states have no land border in the data, being islands or groups of islands: Bahrain, Japan, Maldives, Philippines, Singapore. The languages listed for the most places here are English (26), Arabic (13), Russian (8), Chinese (7). Clocks in the region run from UTC+2:00 to UTC+9:00, a spread of 7 hours.
