Standard, reduced, zero and exempt VAT

The Commission's database groups rates into types:

  • Standard rate: the normal rate on most goods and services.
  • Reduced rate: a lower rate for listed categories such as food, books, passenger transport or hotels.
  • Super-reduced rate: a rate below the lowest reduced rate that some countries have kept.
  • Parking rate: an intermediate rate (at least 12% under the rules) that some countries applied to goods that were previously taxed at a reduced rate.
  • Zero rate and exempt: no VAT is charged. With a zero rate the seller can usually still recover VAT paid on costs; with an exempt supply the seller usually cannot. The database lists both under the exempt label.

The detail of what counts and how recovery works is national law; this site shows the entries and comments only. (The explanation of zero versus exempt is general knowledge, not read from the database.)
