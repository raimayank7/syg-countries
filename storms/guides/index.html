Storm data guides

How to use this page

The three guides explain how to read the storm tables. One explains what the NOAA Storm Events Database contains, what one row means and why a report is not the same as a storm. One explains how to read the damage estimates, why they are not insurance claims and why they cannot be compared across years without adjusting for inflation. One explains the Enhanced Fujita scale, which rates tornado damage and not the wind itself. Read them before you compare two counties or two years.

  • What the Storm Events Database is

    What the database contains, what one row means, why reports are not storms, and what the numbers can and cannot show.

  • How to read storm damage estimates

    What the damage figures in storm databases mean, why they are not insurance claims and why they are not comparable across years without adjusting for inflation.

  • The Enhanced Fujita (EF) scale

    What the EF0 to EF5 ratings mean, that they rate damage and not the wind itself, and how to read tornado counts.

Data: NOAA NCEI Storm Events Database, 1996 to 2025, read 14 November 2026. Counts are of reports, not storms; damage figures are estimates and are not adjusted for inflation. Independent site, not connected with NOAA or the National Weather Service.
