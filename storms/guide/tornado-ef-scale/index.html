The Enhanced Fujita (EF) scale

Tornadoes in the United States are rated after the fact on the Enhanced Fujita scale, from EF0 (weakest) to EF5 (strongest). The rating is based on the damage found, which is then linked to the likely wind speed that caused it, not on a wind measurement. The usual wind-speed bands are about 65 to 85 mph for EF0, 86 to 110 for EF1, 111 to 135 for EF2, 136 to 165 for EF3, 166 to 200 for EF4, and over 200 for EF5.

How to read tornado counts

Most tornadoes are rated EF0 or EF1. A tornado with no buildings in its path can be rated low even if it was strong. The tornado page shows reports by state and year. The numbers of reports have risen in part because more are noticed and reported than decades ago. (The EF bands are general knowledge, not read from the database files.)
