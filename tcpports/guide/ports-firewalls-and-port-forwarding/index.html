Ports, firewalls and port forwarding

A firewall decides which connections may pass. Most are set by port and protocol: allow inbound 443 to the web server, block everything else. Home routers also hide your devices behind one public address (network address translation), which blocks unsolicited inbound connections by default.

Port forwarding

If you want to reach a device at home from outside, such as a camera, a game server or a computer, you can tell the router to forward a port: connections to the router's public address on that port go to a chosen device inside. It is a deliberate hole in the wall. Forward only the port you need, to one device, and think about whether a VPN to your home network would be safer than a forwarded port.

Good habits

  1. Open nothing you do not need, and close what you no longer use.
  2. Keep software that listens on a port up to date; open ports are where attackers look first.
  3. Prefer encrypted services (SSH, HTTPS, IMAPS) to their plain twins.
  4. Never expose databases, remote desktop, file sharing or admin interfaces directly to the internet; put them behind a VPN or limit them to known addresses.
  5. Change default passwords on every device that listens on a port.
  6. Check what is open: see how to check.

The well-known ports pages note the usual cautions for each service. These are general habits, not a security audit of your own system.
