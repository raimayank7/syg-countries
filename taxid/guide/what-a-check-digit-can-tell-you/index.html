What a check digit can and cannot tell you

Most tax and business numbers end with one or two check digits: digits worked out from all the others by a fixed rule. If someone mistypes a digit or swaps two, the rule usually fails, so a program can catch the slip without looking anything up.

What it catches

  • One wrong digit (nearly always).
  • Two neighbouring digits swapped (in many systems).
  • The wrong length or impossible characters.

What it cannot catch

  • A number that was never issued but happens to pass: with a one-digit check, about one in ten random numbers pass.
  • A number that was issued and later cancelled.
  • A real number that belongs to a different business than the one you are dealing with.

What to do

  1. Check the shape here to catch typing mistakes.
  2. Confirm with the official register (each ID page links one): the name and address it shows should match the business.
  3. For business payments, confirm bank details separately, by calling a number you already trust.

Checking a number is a small part of dealing with a new supplier; it does not replace other checks.
