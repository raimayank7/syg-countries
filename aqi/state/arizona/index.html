Air quality in Arizona by county

The EPA's annual summaries have data for 15 counties in Arizona that have, or had, an air monitor. In 2025, 13 of them have a summary; their median AQIs run from 13 to 73 and the highest daily AQI in any of them was 1215. Only counties with a monitor appear; a county missing here has no monitor in the data, which says nothing about its air.

How the state looks over time

The median share of Good days among the counties that had a nearly complete year (at least 200 days with an index) was, in 1990: 69% of days Good in the median county (5 counties); 2000: 58% of days Good in the median county (6 counties); 2010: 65% of days Good in the median county (11 counties); 2020: 74% of days Good in the median county (13 counties); 2025: 66% of days Good in the median county (13 counties). The first year with any county summary in Arizona is 1980, and the year with the most monitored counties is 2007 (14 of 15). The number of monitored counties changed over the years, so a change in a median can come from a change in which counties had monitors.

2025 in Arizona

Among the counties with a nearly complete year, the highest share of Good days was in Navajo (99.5%) and the lowest in Maricopa (8.8%). Across the 13 counties with a 2025 summary, ozone set the day's index on 53% of the counted days, fine particles (PM2.5) on 16% and other pollutants on 30%. The highest single-day index was 1215, in Pinal, and 9 of the 13 counties had at least one day in the Unhealthy, Very unhealthy or Hazardous categories. A high maximum is often one event, such as wildfire smoke or dust, so read it together with the median. The share of Good days compares counties only roughly, because monitors measure different pollutants for different parts of the year.
