Air quality in the US in 1990

The EPA's annual summary for 1990 covers 833 monitored counties. The median county had 81.9% good days and a median daily AQI of 35; in all, 3419 days in the Unhealthy, Very unhealthy or Hazardous categories were counted across those counties (one bad day in three counties counts as three).

What set the index in 1990

Across all county summaries of 1990, the pollutant that set the day's index was ozone on 61% of counted days, coarse particles (PM10) on 16% of counted days, nitrogen dioxide on 13% of counted days, carbon monoxide on 10% of counted days, fine particles (PM2.5) on 1% of counted days. About 47% of the counties (395 of 833) had at least one day in the Unhealthy, Very unhealthy or Hazardous categories, and 48% had an index for 300 or more days, so the rest describe an incomplete year. The summaries come from monitors in 54 states and territories; the most monitored counties are in California (51), Virginia (42), Ohio (41).

In 1989 the median county had 79.5% good days and a median AQI of 36 across 741 monitored counties; In 1980 the median county had 72.4% good days and a median AQI of 39 across 462 monitored counties. Because the set of monitored counties changes from year to year, these comparisons are rough; the weather of a single year, and smoke or dust events, can move the figures either way.
