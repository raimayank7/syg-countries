Air quality in the US in 2008

The EPA's annual summary for 2008 covers 1074 monitored counties. The median county had 66.3% good days and a median daily AQI of 44; in all, 1316 days in the Unhealthy, Very unhealthy or Hazardous categories were counted across those counties (one bad day in three counties counts as three).

What set the index in 2008

Across all county summaries of 2008, the pollutant that set the day's index was ozone on 48% of counted days, fine particles (PM2.5) on 42% of counted days, coarse particles (PM10) on 6% of counted days, nitrogen dioxide on 3% of counted days, carbon monoxide on 1% of counted days. About 24% of the counties (262 of 1,074) had at least one day in the Unhealthy, Very unhealthy or Hazardous categories, and 60% had an index for 300 or more days, so the rest describe an incomplete year. The summaries come from monitors in 53 states and territories; the most monitored counties are in California (53), North Carolina (47), Texas (43).

In 2007 the median county had 61.0% good days and a median AQI of 45 across 1079 monitored counties; In 1998 the median county had 76.7% good days and a median AQI of 38 across 968 monitored counties. Because the set of monitored counties changes from year to year, these comparisons are rough; the weather of a single year, and smoke or dust events, can move the figures either way.
