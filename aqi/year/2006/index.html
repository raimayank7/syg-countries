Air quality in the US in 2006

The EPA's annual summary for 2006 covers 1088 monitored counties. The median county had 65.2% good days and a median daily AQI of 44; in all, 1988 days in the Unhealthy, Very unhealthy or Hazardous categories were counted across those counties (one bad day in three counties counts as three).

What set the index in 2006

Across all county summaries of 2006, the pollutant that set the day's index was ozone on 51% of counted days, fine particles (PM2.5) on 37% of counted days, coarse particles (PM10) on 7% of counted days, nitrogen dioxide on 4% of counted days, carbon monoxide on 1% of counted days. About 36% of the counties (393 of 1,088) had at least one day in the Unhealthy, Very unhealthy or Hazardous categories, and 56% had an index for 300 or more days, so the rest describe an incomplete year. The summaries come from monitors in 54 states and territories; the most monitored counties are in California (53), North Carolina (48), Ohio (40).

In 2005 the median county had 63.0% good days and a median AQI of 44 across 1111 monitored counties; In 1996 the median county had 84.0% good days and a median AQI of 35 across 988 monitored counties. Because the set of monitored counties changes from year to year, these comparisons are rough; the weather of a single year, and smoke or dust events, can move the figures either way.
