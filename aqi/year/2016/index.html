Air quality in the US in 2016

The EPA's annual summary for 2016 covers 1029 monitored counties. The median county had 78.5% good days and a median daily AQI of 41; in all, 519 days in the Unhealthy, Very unhealthy or Hazardous categories were counted across those counties (one bad day in three counties counts as three).

What set the index in 2016

Across all county summaries of 2016, the pollutant that set the day's index was ozone on 51% of counted days, fine particles (PM2.5) on 44% of counted days, coarse particles (PM10) on 4% of counted days, nitrogen dioxide on 1% of counted days, carbon monoxide on 0% of counted days. About 14% of the counties (147 of 1,029) had at least one day in the Unhealthy, Very unhealthy or Hazardous categories, and 72% had an index for 300 or more days, so the rest describe an incomplete year. The summaries come from monitors in 53 states and territories; the most monitored counties are in California (54), Texas (42), Florida (39).

In 2015 the median county had 75.6% good days and a median AQI of 41 across 1041 monitored counties; In 2006 the median county had 65.2% good days and a median AQI of 44 across 1088 monitored counties. Because the set of monitored counties changes from year to year, these comparisons are rough; the weather of a single year, and smoke or dust events, can move the figures either way.
