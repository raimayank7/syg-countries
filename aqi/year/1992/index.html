Air quality in the US in 1992

The EPA's annual summary for 1992 covers 909 monitored counties. The median county had 86.1% good days and a median daily AQI of 33; in all, 2362 days in the Unhealthy, Very unhealthy or Hazardous categories were counted across those counties (one bad day in three counties counts as three).

What set the index in 1992

Across all county summaries of 1992, the pollutant that set the day's index was ozone on 60% of counted days, coarse particles (PM10) on 16% of counted days, nitrogen dioxide on 13% of counted days, carbon monoxide on 9% of counted days, fine particles (PM2.5) on 1% of counted days. About 42% of the counties (386 of 909) had at least one day in the Unhealthy, Very unhealthy or Hazardous categories, and 47% had an index for 300 or more days, so the rest describe an incomplete year. The summaries come from monitors in 53 states and territories; the most monitored counties are in California (51), Ohio (47), Virginia (43).

In 1991 the median county had 81.0% good days and a median AQI of 35 across 877 monitored counties; In 1982 the median county had 74.9% good days and a median AQI of 39 across 487 monitored counties. Because the set of monitored counties changes from year to year, these comparisons are rough; the weather of a single year, and smoke or dust events, can move the figures either way.
