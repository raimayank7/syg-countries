How many earthquakes happen each year?

Earthquakes happen all the time. Most are too small to feel. The ones that make news are the large ones, and their numbers can be counted from the catalog on this site, which lists every event of magnitude 5.0 and above.

The counts

Over the 30 full years from 1996 to 2025, an average year in the catalog has:

MagnitudePer year, on average
5 or more1,711
6 or more148
7 or more15
8 or more1

The numbers go up and down from year to year. The strongest of the 30 years had 24 earthquakes of magnitude 7 or more, and the weakest 7. Counts of large earthquakes do not follow a calendar: a great earthquake can be followed by thousands of aftershocks that add to the totals of its year.

What about smaller ones?

Below magnitude 5 the number rises quickly: each step down the scale brings about ten times as many earthquakes. USGS and other agencies record many thousands of smaller earthquakes each year, but the catalog on this site stops at 5.0 to keep the lists useful.

Is the number growing?

The catalog shows more events in recent decades, but that is due to many more instruments. For great earthquakes of magnitude 8 or more, which were always recorded, there is no clear upward trend in the numbers: see the table on the page by year.

Written for this site; counts are computed from the USGS catalog (to 12 November 2026).
