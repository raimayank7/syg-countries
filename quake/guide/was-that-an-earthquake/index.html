Was that an earthquake?

If the floor shook, the first question is whether it was an earthquake. A few minutes later the answer is on the live list of this site, which shows earthquakes of magnitude 4.5 or more from the USGS feed (the list is refreshed every five minutes while the page is open).

How to check

  1. Open the live list and set the period to "Past day".
  2. Look for an event near you and near the time you felt it. The list shows the time in your own time zone.
  3. If nothing is listed, the earthquake was probably smaller than 4.5, or still being processed: agencies take some minutes to publish. A smaller earthquake can be felt strongly if it is shallow and close.
  4. Check the USGS map and your national agency, which list smaller events and may have a faster local report.

Report that you felt it

USGS runs a "Did You Feel It?" service at earthquake.usgs.gov, where people who felt an earthquake describe what they experienced; the reports are used to draw maps of the shaking. Your national seismological agency usually has a similar form.

Other things that shake a house

Heavy lorries, blasting at quarries, building demolition, and sonic booms can shake a building. A short, sharp bump followed by silence is often an explosion or a heavy vehicle; an earthquake usually starts with a smaller shake and builds, rolling, for several seconds. If you are not sure, the listing is the test.

This site cannot give emergency advice. In a real emergency call your local emergency number and follow your authorities.
