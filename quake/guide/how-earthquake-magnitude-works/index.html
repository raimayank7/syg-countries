How earthquake magnitude works

Magnitude is a number that says how big an earthquake is at its source. It is not how hard the ground shakes where you stand: that is called intensity and it depends on distance, depth, the ground you are on and the buildings. One earthquake has one magnitude and many intensities.

The scale is logarithmic

Each whole number up the scale means the recorded ground motion is about 10 times larger and the energy released is about 32 times larger. So a magnitude 7 releases about 32 times the energy of a magnitude 6 and about 1,000 times that of a magnitude 5. This is why the numbers look close while the earthquakes are very different, and why a magnitude 8 is so rare: in the catalog on this site, a typical recent year has about 148 of magnitude 6 or more, 15 of magnitude 7 or more and 1 of magnitude 8 or more.

Why there are several magnitudes

Over the last century scientists have used different ways to compute the number: from body waves (mb), surface waves (Ms), and, for large earthquakes, the moment magnitude (Mw), which comes from the size of the rupture and the slip and does not saturate for the biggest events. The catalog shows one value for each event, taken from the method that USGS prefers for it. For today's earthquakes above about magnitude 5.5 that is mostly a moment magnitude. For old ones it is whatever could be worked out, which is why magnitudes of early decades are approximate and sometimes revised.

Reading a magnitude

  • Under 4: usually felt only near the epicentre, if at all.
  • 4 to 5: felt widely; damage is possible only for very vulnerable buildings close by.
  • 5 to 6: can damage poorly built structures near the source.
  • 6 to 7: can be destructive within tens of kilometres.
  • 7 and above: major; can damage a wide area, and great earthquakes (8 and above) can be felt over huge distances and can cause tsunamis if they strike under the sea.

These are only rough guides: a shallow magnitude 6 under a city is far more dangerous than a deep magnitude 7 far from people.
