Earthquakes of 1956: the strongest and how many

243 earthquakes of magnitude 5 and above in the USGS catalog.

The USGS catalog lists 243 earthquakes of magnitude 5.0 or more in 1956: 134 of magnitude 5 to 5.9, 104 of 6 to 6.9, 5 of 7 to 7.9 and 0 of 8 or more. The strongest was magnitude 7.7 on 9 July 1956, listed as "19 km SSE of Amorgós, Greece".

The most affected areas by number of listed earthquakes were Mexico (24), Japan (19), Greece (18), Russia (17), Alaska (12), Tonga (9). The busiest month was February with 38 earthquakes. For comparison, the catalog counts about 1,711 earthquakes of magnitude 5 or more a year since 1996. Fewer were recorded in 1956, but not because the Earth was quieter: instruments were scarce, so smaller earthquakes went unrecorded. Magnitudes of 1956 are estimates made from old instrument records and later re-evaluated; treat them as approximate.

Month by month the catalog lists: January 20; February 38; March 19; April 12; May 15; June 23; July 25; August 18; September 17; October 21; November 16; December 19. Compared with 1955, which has 227 listed earthquakes of magnitude 5 or more, that is 16 more. The earthquakes of magnitude 7 or more, in date order: 18 February 1956 M7.3 Izu Islands, Japan region; 23 May 1956 M7.6 144 km SSW of Leava, Wallis and Futuna; 9 June 1956 M7.3 42 km NNW of (name unreadable in source), Afghanistan; 9 July 1956 M7.7 19 km SSE of Amorgós, Greece; 24 October 1956 M7.1 12 km SSW of Masachapa, Nicaragua.
