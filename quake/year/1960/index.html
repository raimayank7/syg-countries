Earthquakes of 1960: the strongest and how many

286 earthquakes of magnitude 5 and above in the USGS catalog.

The USGS catalog lists 286 earthquakes of magnitude 5.0 or more in 1960: 150 of magnitude 5 to 5.9, 123 of 6 to 6.9, 11 of 7 to 7.9 and 2 of 8 or more. The strongest was magnitude 9.5 on 22 May 1960, listed as "1960 Great Chilean Earthquake (Valdivia Earthquake)". It was one of 2 earthquakes of magnitude 8 or more that year.

The most affected areas by number of listed earthquakes were Chile (43), Japan (23), Alaska (17), Indonesia (16), Russia (14), Papua New Guinea (14). The busiest month was March with 33 earthquakes. For comparison, the catalog counts about 1,711 earthquakes of magnitude 5 or more a year since 1996. Fewer were recorded in 1960, but not because the Earth was quieter: instruments were scarce, so smaller earthquakes went unrecorded. Magnitudes of 1960 are estimates made from old instrument records and later re-evaluated; treat them as approximate.

Month by month the catalog lists: January 25; February 16; March 33; April 11; May 33; June 30; July 27; August 19; September 29; October 22; November 22; December 19. Compared with 1959, which has 351 listed earthquakes of magnitude 5 or more, that is 65 fewer.
