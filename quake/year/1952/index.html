Earthquakes of 1952: the strongest and how many

338 earthquakes of magnitude 5 and above in the USGS catalog.

The USGS catalog lists 338 earthquakes of magnitude 5.0 or more in 1952: 172 of magnitude 5 to 5.9, 160 of 6 to 6.9, 4 of 7 to 7.9 and 2 of 8 or more. The strongest was magnitude 9.0 on 4 November 1952, listed as "89 km ESE of Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky, Russia". It was one of 2 earthquakes of magnitude 8 or more that year.

The most affected areas by number of listed earthquakes were Russia (69), Japan (41), China (22), California (21), Greece (14), Papua New Guinea (12). The busiest month was November with 69 earthquakes. For comparison, the catalog counts about 1,711 earthquakes of magnitude 5 or more a year since 1996. Fewer were recorded in 1952, but not because the Earth was quieter: instruments were scarce, so smaller earthquakes went unrecorded. Magnitudes of 1952 are estimates made from old instrument records and later re-evaluated; treat them as approximate.

Month by month the catalog lists: January 12; February 12; March 42; April 24; May 18; June 28; July 30; August 19; September 16; October 41; November 69; December 27. Compared with 1951, which has 260 listed earthquakes of magnitude 5 or more, that is 78 more. The earthquakes of magnitude 7 or more, in date order: 14 February 1952 M7.0 77 km N of Baukau, Timor Leste; 4 March 1952 M8.1 1952 Tokachi-Oki Earthquake; 19 March 1952 M7.3 108 km ENE of Cortes, Philippines; 21 July 1952 M7.5 The 1952 Kern County, California Earthquake; 17 August 1952 M7.4 101 km SSW of Nagqu, China; 4 November 1952 M9.0 89 km ESE of Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky, Russia.
