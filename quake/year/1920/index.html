Earthquakes of 1920: the strongest and how many

99 earthquakes of magnitude 5 and above in the USGS catalog.

The USGS catalog lists 99 earthquakes of magnitude 5.0 or more in 1920: 30 of magnitude 5 to 5.9, 62 of 6 to 6.9, 5 of 7 to 7.9 and 2 of 8 or more. The strongest was magnitude 8.2 on 5 June 1920, listed as "1920 Hualien, Taiwan Earthquake". It was one of 2 earthquakes of magnitude 8 or more that year.

The most affected areas by number of listed earthquakes were Greece (8), Mexico (6), Japan (5), Papua New Guinea (5), China (5), Indonesia (4). The busiest month was September with 13 earthquakes. For comparison, the catalog counts about 1,711 earthquakes of magnitude 5 or more a year since 1996. Fewer were recorded in 1920, but not because the Earth was quieter: instruments were scarce, so smaller earthquakes went unrecorded. Magnitudes of 1920 are estimates made from old instrument records and later re-evaluated; treat them as approximate.

Month by month the catalog lists: January 8; February 9; March 6; April 4; May 11; June 2; July 9; August 9; September 13; October 12; November 5; December 11. Compared with 1919, which has 110 listed earthquakes of magnitude 5 or more, that is 11 fewer. The earthquakes of magnitude 7 or more, in date order: 2 February 1920 M7.8 74 km SSW of Kokopo, Papua New Guinea; 5 June 1920 M8.2 1920 Hualien, Taiwan Earthquake; 3 August 1920 M7.0 32 km NW of Fiambalá, Argentina; 20 September 1920 M8.1 1920 Vanuatu Earthquake; 18 October 1920 M7.1 147 km E of Kuril’sk, Russia; 10 December 1920 M7.3 50 km SSW of Carahue, Chile; 16 December 1920 M7.9 125 km E of Baiyin, China.
