Earthquakes of 1906: the strongest and how many

94 earthquakes of magnitude 5 and above in the USGS catalog.

The USGS catalog lists 94 earthquakes of magnitude 5.0 or more in 1906: 16 of magnitude 5 to 5.9, 56 of 6 to 6.9, 18 of 7 to 7.9 and 4 of 8 or more. The strongest was magnitude 8.8 on 31 January 1906, listed as "1906 Ecuador-Colombia Earthquake". It was one of 4 earthquakes of magnitude 8 or more that year.

The most affected areas by number of listed earthquakes were Japan (10), Loyalty Islands (5), Chile (5), Alaska (4), India (4), Taiwan (4). The busiest month was March with 11 earthquakes. For comparison, the catalog counts about 1,711 earthquakes of magnitude 5 or more a year since 1996. Fewer were recorded in 1906, but not because the Earth was quieter: instruments were scarce, so smaller earthquakes went unrecorded. Magnitudes of 1906 are estimates made from old instrument records and later re-evaluated; treat them as approximate.

Month by month the catalog lists: January 8; February 6; March 11; April 9; May 6; June 7; July 5; August 10; September 5; October 11; November 7; December 9. Compared with 1905, which has 75 listed earthquakes of magnitude 5 or more, that is 19 more.
