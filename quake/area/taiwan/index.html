Earthquakes in Taiwan: biggest, latest and history

1,038 earthquakes of magnitude 5 and above in the USGS catalog since 1906.

The catalog lists 1,038 earthquakes of magnitude 5.0 or more whose place name ends in "Taiwan", from 1906 to 2026. 210 of them are of magnitude 6 to 6.9, 26 of magnitude 7 to 7.9 and 0 of magnitude 8 or more. The strongest is magnitude 7.8 on 24 November 1951, listed as "Taiwan". The most recent one in the catalog is magnitude 5.5 on 25 August 2026.

The busiest decade is the 2020s, with 164 listed earthquakes. Part of such a difference is real activity (aftershocks of a great earthquake can fill a decade with entries) and part is a matter of instruments: records of older decades are less complete. Since 1996, the catalog averages about 15.2 earthquakes of magnitude 5 or more a year for this area.

1,038 of the earthquakes have a depth in the catalog. 95% of them are shallow (under 70 km, the kind most likely to be felt strongly at the surface), 5% are intermediate (70 to 300 km) and 0% are deep (300 km or more). The depth of an earthquake is as important as its size: a deep earthquake of the same magnitude shakes the surface less than a shallow one.

Common questions

What was the biggest earthquake in Taiwan?

In the catalog, the strongest earthquake named for Taiwan is magnitude 7.8 on 24 November 1951 (Taiwan).

How often do earthquakes of magnitude 5 or more happen near Taiwan?

Since 1996 the catalog has about 15.2 per year with this name (magnitude 5 and above). Earlier decades are less complete.

Are these all the earthquakes there?

No. Only magnitude 5.0 and above are included, and the early part of the catalog is incomplete. Many smaller earthquakes happen that are not listed.

Where does the information come from?

From the USGS earthquake catalog. For the full record, maps and reports go to earthquake.usgs.gov.

Data: U.S. Geological Survey, ANSS ComCat catalog, to 12 November 2026. The area is the last part of the place name USGS gives. Magnitudes are of mixed types and earlier records are less complete. For the real-time list see the live list. This site is independent and not part of the USGS.
