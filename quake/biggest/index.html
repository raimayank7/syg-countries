The biggest earthquakes ever recorded: top 100 since 1900

Ranked by magnitude in the USGS catalog.

The strongest earthquake in the catalog is magnitude 9.5, on 22 May 1960, listed as "1960 Great Chilean Earthquake (Valdivia Earthquake)". 100 earthquakes of magnitude 8 or more are listed since 1900; the top of the list is dominated by subduction zones, where one plate dives under another: the coasts of Chile, Alaska, Sumatra, Japan and Kamchatka. The table below lists the 100 strongest. Ties are shown by date.

A caution on the ranking: the catalog shows one magnitude for each event, and for the biggest ones scientists have re-calculated the figures several times, so different sources can give slightly different values (for example 9.5 or 9.4 for the 1960 earthquake in Chile). The depth is the depth of the start of the rupture, and a great earthquake ruptures for hundreds of kilometres.
