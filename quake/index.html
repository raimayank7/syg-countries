How to read the list

The number on the left is the magnitude: a measure of the size of the earthquake at its source. Each whole number is about 32 times more energy, so a 7 is far bigger than a 6 (how magnitude works). Next to the place you see when it happened and how deep it was; deeper earthquakes shake the surface less (depth explained). The list is read by your browser straight from the USGS feed, and a tap on a row opens the USGS page for that earthquake with maps and reports. New earthquakes can take several minutes to appear, and USGS may adjust the magnitude later.
