Why the country of an IP address is not where someone is

The country in these files is the country of the organisation that first received a block. It is not where the address is in use, and it never says where a person is.

  • Blocks are sold and leased. A company in one country can lease a block to customers in another; the record keeps the first country.
  • Providers span countries. A multinational runs one block across several. Mobile networks and VPN services use blocks that say nothing about the person behind them.
  • Addresses are shared. Many people sit behind one address through a router or mobile carrier.
  • Registries say so. APNIC states in its file that its reports show where resources were first allocated and are not an authoritative statement of where one is used.

What this means in practice

Treat country-by-address data as a rough guess. Blocking a country by its ranges stops some legitimate users and lets others through. Commercial "geolocation" databases add other sources and still make mistakes. Do not use any such data to judge a person.

The United States page shows this: its 1,606,587,040 addresses were first allocated there, but it says nothing about who uses them now.
