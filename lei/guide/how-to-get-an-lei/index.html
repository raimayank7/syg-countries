How to get an LEI

An LEI is not issued by GLEIF itself but by accredited Local Operating Units (LOUs). The steps are similar everywhere; prices, speed and forms differ between issuers, so compare a few before applying.

  1. Find out whether you need one. The party that asks for it (a bank, a broker, a regulator) will say which rule applies and whether a particular LOU is required.
  2. Choose an issuer. GLEIF publishes the list of accredited LOUs on its website. Fees and renewal prices are set by each LOU.
  3. Prepare the details of the entity: the full legal name exactly as registered, the registration number and the register in which it is registered, the legal form, the registered address and headquarters address, and the contact details of the person applying. Many issuers ask for proof of authority.
  4. Apply and pay. The issuer checks the details against the official business register and issues the code once they match.
  5. Look up the new record with the checker on this site, to confirm that the name and address are right.
  6. Renew on time. Set a reminder; a lapsed LEI is still shown but marked as not renewed.

This site does not issue LEIs and gives no advice on regulations. Ask your adviser or the party requiring the LEI which rules apply to you.
