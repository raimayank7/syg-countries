Countries of Africa

Africa is the second largest continent by both area and population. It stretches from the Mediterranean Sea in the north to the meeting of the Atlantic and Indian oceans in the south, and the equator runs through its middle. It has more countries than any other continent.

The region in numbers

This region has 59 entries on this site, of which 54 are member states of the United Nations. Together they hold about 1.55 billion people, roughly 19 percent of the world total, on about 30.4 million square kilometres of land. Nigeria has the most people (about 238 million). Algeria is the largest by area (2,381,740 km²), and Seychelles is the smallest member state (455 km²). 16 of its member states are landlocked: Botswana, Burkina Faso, Burundi, Central African Republic, Chad, Eswatini, Ethiopia, Lesotho, Malawi, Mali, Niger, Rwanda, South Sudan, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe.

Rich and poor, crowded and empty

By GDP per person among member states, the highest figures in the data are Seychelles ($19,400), Mauritius ($13,000), Gabon ($8,300), and the lowest are Burundi ($230), Central African Republic ($560), Madagascar ($600). A baby can expect the longest life in Tunisia (76.7 years) and the shortest in Nigeria (54.6 years), a gap of 22 years inside one region. The most crowded member state is Mauritius, with about 610 people to each square kilometre, and the emptiest is Namibia, with about 3.7. Figures are World Bank values for the year shown on each country page.

Borders, languages, money and time

DR Congo has the most land neighbours in the region, 9 of them. 6 member states have no land border in the data, being islands or groups of islands: Cape Verde, Comoros, Madagascar, Mauritius, Seychelles, São Tomé and Príncipe. The languages listed for the most places here are French (28), English (27), Arabic (15), Portuguese (6). The most widely shared money is the West African CFA franc, the main currency of 8 entries. Clocks in the region run from UTC-1:00 to UTC+6:00, a spread of 7 hours.
