Countries of Europe

Europe is a small continent with many countries. It is joined to Asia in the east, where the Ural Mountains are usually taken as the border, and is bounded by the Atlantic Ocean in the west and the Mediterranean Sea in the south.

The region in numbers

This region has 53 entries on this site, of which 44 are member states of the United Nations. Together they hold about 746 million people, roughly 9 percent of the world total, on about 23.1 million square kilometres of land. Russia has the most people (about 144 million). Russia is the largest by area (17,100,000 km²), and Monaco is the smallest member state (1.95 km²). 13 of its member states are landlocked: Andorra, Austria, Belarus, Czechia, Hungary, Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, Moldova, North Macedonia, San Marino, Serbia, Slovakia, Switzerland.

Rich and poor, crowded and empty

By GDP per person among member states, the highest figures in the data are Monaco ($288,000), Liechtenstein ($220,200), Luxembourg ($147,300), and the lowest are Ukraine ($5,900), Moldova ($8,600), Belarus ($10,300). A baby can expect the longest life in Monaco (86.5 years) and the shortest in Moldova (71.3 years), a gap of 15 years inside one region. The most crowded member state is Monaco, with about 19,662 people to each square kilometre, and the emptiest is Iceland, with about 3.8. Figures are World Bank values for the year shown on each country page.

Borders, languages, money and time

Russia has the most land neighbours in the region, 14 of them. 3 member states have no land border in the data, being islands or groups of islands: Cyprus, Iceland, Malta. The languages listed for the most places here are English (12), German (9), French (9), Russian (8). The most widely shared money is the euro, the main currency of 28 entries. Clocks in the region run from UTC-1:00 to UTC+12:00, a spread of 13 hours.
