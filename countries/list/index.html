About the columns

  • Population: World Bank figures, mostly for 2025. A few small territories use a GeoNames figure.
  • Area: total area in square kilometres, from GeoNames.
  • People per km²: population divided by area, worked out by us.
  • GDP per person: the value of everything the economy produces in a year, divided by the population, in current US dollars (World Bank). It is a rough guide to average income, not to how money is shared.

"No value" means the source has no figure. Open a country to see the year of each figure.

What the list shows at a glance

Sorted by population, the first three are India (1.46 billion), China (1.41 billion), United States (342 million). Half of all the people in the world live in just 7 countries, and 16 countries have 100 million people or more. At the other end, 37 member states of the United Nations have fewer than a million people each. The country in the middle of the list, with as many member states above it as below, has about 9.21 million people.

Sorted by area, the three largest countries are Russia (17.1 million km²), Canada (9.98 million km²), United States (9.63 million km²). Size and population do not go together: sort by people per km² and the top places go to small, city-like states such as Monaco and Singapore, while the emptiest member states are Mongolia and Australia.

By GDP per person the list runs from Monaco ($288,000) down to Burundi ($230), so the highest figure is roughly 1,232 times the lowest.

Ways to use the list

  • Type a region such as "Africa" in the filter box, then sort, to rank the countries of one region only.
  • Sort by area and then look at the density column to see which large countries are nearly empty.
  • For two countries in detail, use the compare tool.
