TOI-663 d is a confirmed planet that orbits the star TOI-663. In the archive it is a super-Earth, about 1.92 times the radius of Earth (24,465 km across at its widest, against Earth's 12,742 km), with a mass of about 5.20 Earth masses. It was discovered in 2024 by the transit method, with Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS).

How big is TOI-663 d?

Its radius is 1.92 Earth radii, so it is larger than Earth. With a mass of 5.20 Earth masses it packs 5.20 times Earth's mass into about 7.08 times its volume. The density comes out at 4.00 g/cm3 (Earth: 5.51; water: 1.00). That is in between, like a mix of rock and water or gas.

Orbit and year

It goes once around its star in 7.10 days, so its year is 0.0194 Earth years long. Mercury takes 88 days; Earth takes 365.25. Its average distance from the star is 0.0576 AU, where 1 AU is the distance from the Earth to the Sun; Mercury is at 0.39 AU and Jupiter at 5.2 AU. It is closer to its star than Mercury is to the Sun.

Temperature and starlight

The archive gives an equilibrium temperature of 482 K (209 degrees Celsius). That is the temperature the planet would have if starlight were its only source of heat; it is not the temperature of a surface, and an atmosphere can change it a lot. This is hotter than boiling water. It receives 12.9 times the starlight that Earth gets from the Sun.

The star

The star is TOI-663, with spectral type M1 V, a surface temperature of 3,681 K (the Sun: 5,772 K), a radius of 0.512 times the Sun's, a mass of 0.514 times the Sun's. The system has 3 known planets. The planets of this star: TOI-663 b, TOI-663 c, TOI-663 d.

How far away is it?

The system is about 209 light years (64.2 parsecs) from us. Light from it left 209 years ago. A spacecraft travelling like Voyager 1, at about 17 km per second, would need roughly 3,694,193 years. The star has an apparent magnitude of 13.7: far too faint to see without a large telescope.

Common questions

How was TOI-663 d discovered?

TOI-663 d was discovered in 2024 by the transit method using Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS).

How long is a year on TOI-663 d?

It orbits its star every 7.10 days.

How far is TOI-663 d from Earth?

About 209 light years.

Could life exist on TOI-663 d?

This site does not say. Size, temperature and starlight are only part of what matters; nothing is known about its atmosphere or surface.
