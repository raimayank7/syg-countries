HAT-P-42 b: exoplanet

HAT-P-42 b is a confirmed planet that orbits the star HAT-P-42. In the archive it is a gas giant, about 14.3 times the radius of Earth (182,822 km across at its widest, against Earth's 12,742 km), or 1.28 of Jupiter's radius, with a mass of about 332 Earth masses (1.04 times Jupiter's). It was discovered in 2012 by the transit method, with HATNet.

How big is HAT-P-42 b?

Its radius is 14.3 Earth radii, so it is much larger than Earth. That is 128% of Jupiter's radius. With a mass of 332 Earth masses it packs 332 times Earth's mass into about 2,954 times its volume. The density comes out at 0.61 g/cm3 (Earth: 5.51; water: 1.00). That points to a world with a thick envelope of gas or water, not a bare rock.

Orbit and year

It goes once around its star in 4.64 days, so its year is 0.0127 Earth years long. Mercury takes 88 days; Earth takes 365.25. Its average distance from the star is 0.0575 AU, where 1 AU is the distance from the Earth to the Sun; Mercury is at 0.39 AU and Jupiter at 5.2 AU. It is closer to its star than Mercury is to the Sun.

Temperature and starlight

The archive gives an equilibrium temperature of 1,428 K (1,155 degrees Celsius). That is the temperature the planet would have if starlight were its only source of heat; it is not the temperature of a surface, and an atmosphere can change it a lot. This is hotter than boiling water. It receives 684 times the starlight that Earth gets from the Sun.

The star

The star is HAT-P-42, with a surface temperature of 5,743 K (the Sun: 5,772 K), a radius of 1.53 times the Sun's, a mass of 1.18 times the Sun's. The system has 1 known planet.

How far away is it?

The system is about 1,322 light years (405 parsecs) from us. Light from it left 1,322 years ago. A spacecraft travelling like Voyager 1, at about 17 km per second, would need roughly 23,307,000 years. The star has an apparent magnitude of 12.1: far too faint to see without a large telescope.

Common questions

How was HAT-P-42 b discovered?

HAT-P-42 b was discovered in 2012 by the transit method using HATNet.

How long is a year on HAT-P-42 b?

It orbits its star every 4.64 days.

How far is HAT-P-42 b from Earth?

About 1,322 light years.

Could life exist on HAT-P-42 b?

This site does not say. Size, temperature and starlight are only part of what matters; nothing is known about its atmosphere or surface.
