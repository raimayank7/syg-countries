Exoplanets discovered in 2012

144 new confirmed planets.

The archive lists 144 confirmed planets as discovered in 2012, which brought the total to 783. Most were found by the transit method (94 of them). Others used radial velocity (39), microlensing (7), imaging (2). The facilities credited with most of them were Kepler (59), SuperWASP (23), La Silla Observatory (12), HATNet (8).

The nearest of them is GJ 667 C b, 23.6 light years away; the most massive is KELT-1 b with about 8,654 Earth masses.

What the newly found planets are like

Of the 144 planets, 144 have a measured radius, 144 a measured mass and 136 an orbital period in the archive. The median radius is 12.0 Earth radii (smallest 0.82, largest 23.0). The median orbit takes 11.7 days. The median distance is 1,033 light years and the farthest is 25,179. By the simple size classes used on this site they are: 89 gas giants, 37 Neptune-like planets, 15 super-Earths, 3 Earth-sized planets. They orbit 107 different stars; 33 of those stars have more than one planet in this list. The size classes are a rough sort by radius or mass; the archive records only what was measured, so planets without a measured size are listed as plain planets.

That is 2.2% of all 6,470 confirmed planets in the archive. The year before, 2011, had 141. Discovery counts rise and fall with the telescopes in operation and with the years in which large batches of candidates were confirmed together, so a single year says little about the pace of the field.
