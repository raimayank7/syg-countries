Exoplanets discovered in 2005

36 new confirmed planets.

The archive lists 36 confirmed planets as discovered in 2005, which brought the total to 172. Most were found by the radial velocity method (33 of them). Others used microlensing (2), imaging (1). The facilities credited with most of them were W. M. Keck Observatory (13), La Silla Observatory (11), Haute-Provence Observatory (4), OGLE (2).

The nearest of them is GJ 876 d, 15.2 light years away; the most massive is AB Pic b with about 4,290 Earth masses.

What the newly found planets are like

Of the 36 planets, 36 have a measured radius, 36 a measured mass and 34 an orbital period in the archive. The median radius is 12.7 Earth radii (smallest 2.21, largest 14.3). The median orbit takes 108 days. The median distance is 147 light years and the farthest is 21,526. By the simple size classes used on this site they are: 31 gas giants, 5 Neptune-like planets. They orbit 36 different stars. The size classes are a rough sort by radius or mass; the archive records only what was measured, so planets without a measured size are listed as plain planets.

That is 0.6% of all 6,470 confirmed planets in the archive. The year before, 2004, had 27. Discovery counts rise and fall with the telescopes in operation and with the years in which large batches of candidates were confirmed together, so a single year says little about the pace of the field.
