Exoplanets discovered in 2023

323 new confirmed planets.

The archive lists 323 confirmed planets as discovered in 2023, which brought the total to 5,584. Most were found by the transit method (225 of them). Others used radial velocity (56), microlensing (30), imaging (7). The facilities credited with most of them were Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) (140), Kepler (73), KMTNet (23), Multiple Observatories (18).

The nearest of them is HN Lib b, 20.4 light years away; the most massive is KMT-2018-BLG-0885L b with about 9,217 Earth masses.

What the newly found planets are like

Of the 323 planets, 322 have a measured radius, 320 a measured mass and 292 an orbital period in the archive. The median radius is 3.76 Earth radii (smallest 0.51, largest 29.3). The median orbit takes 10.9 days. The median distance is 605 light years and the farthest is 25,147. By the simple size classes used on this site they are: 139 gas giants, 113 Neptune-like planets, 49 super-Earths, 21 Earth-sized planets. They orbit 280 different stars; 35 of those stars have more than one planet in this list. The size classes are a rough sort by radius or mass; the archive records only what was measured, so planets without a measured size are listed as plain planets.

That is 5.0% of all 6,470 confirmed planets in the archive. The year before, 2022, had 367. Discovery counts rise and fall with the telescopes in operation and with the years in which large batches of candidates were confirmed together, so a single year says little about the pace of the field.
