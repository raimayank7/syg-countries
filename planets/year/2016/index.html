Exoplanets discovered in 2016

1,504 new confirmed planets.

The archive lists 1,504 confirmed planets as discovered in 2016, which brought the total to 3,442. Most were found by the transit method (1,432 of them). Others used radial velocity (58), microlensing (10), imaging (2). The facilities credited with most of them were Kepler (1276), K2 (103), Multiple Observatories (20), HATSouth (16).

The nearest of them is Proxima Cen b, 4.24 light years away; the most massive is Kepler-80 f with about 12,652 Earth masses.

What the newly found planets are like

Of the 1,504 planets, 1,504 have a measured radius, 1,488 a measured mass and 1,493 an orbital period in the archive. The median radius is 2.21 Earth radii (smallest 0.52, largest 23.2). The median orbit takes 10.1 days. The median distance is 2,442 light years and the farthest is 26,745. By the simple size classes used on this site they are: 671 Neptune-like planets, 437 super-Earths, 204 Earth-sized planets, 192 gas giants. They orbit 1,395 different stars; 90 of those stars have more than one planet in this list. The size classes are a rough sort by radius or mass; the archive records only what was measured, so planets without a measured size are listed as plain planets.

That is 23.2% of all 6,470 confirmed planets in the archive. The year before, 2015, had 155. Discovery counts rise and fall with the telescopes in operation and with the years in which large batches of candidates were confirmed together, so a single year says little about the pace of the field.
