Exoplanets discovered in 1992

2 new confirmed planets.

The archive lists 2 confirmed planets as discovered in 1992, which brought the total to 2. Most were found by the pulsar timing method (2 of them). The facilities credited with most of them were Arecibo Observatory (2).

The nearest of them is PSR B1257+12 d, 1,957 light years away; the most massive is PSR B1257+12 c with about 4.30 Earth masses.

What the newly found planets are like

Of the 2 planets, 2 have a measured radius, 2 a measured mass and 2 an orbital period in the archive. The median radius is 1.85 Earth radii (smallest 1.80, largest 1.91). The median orbit takes 82.4 days. The median distance is 1,957 light years and the farthest is 1,957. By the simple size classes used on this site they are: 2 super-Earths. They orbit 1 different stars; 1 of those stars have more than one planet in this list. The size classes are a rough sort by radius or mass; the archive records only what was measured, so planets without a measured size are listed as plain planets.

That is 0.0% of all 6,470 confirmed planets in the archive. It is the first year in the archive. Discovery counts rise and fall with the telescopes in operation and with the years in which large batches of candidates were confirmed together, so a single year says little about the pace of the field.
