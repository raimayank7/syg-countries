Exoplanets discovered in 2020

234 new confirmed planets.

The archive lists 234 confirmed planets as discovered in 2020, which brought the total to 4,330. Most were found by the transit method (164 of them). Others used radial velocity (46), microlensing (20), imaging (3). The facilities credited with most of them were Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) (68), Kepler (56), K2 (20), Multiple Observatories (18).

The nearest of them is GJ 887 c, 10.7 light years away; the most massive is HD 81817 b with about 7,669 Earth masses.

What the newly found planets are like

Of the 234 planets, 233 have a measured radius, 234 a measured mass and 212 an orbital period in the archive. The median radius is 3.03 Earth radii (smallest 0.903, largest 22.8). The median orbit takes 10.4 days. The median distance is 575 light years and the farthest is 24,788. By the simple size classes used on this site they are: 105 Neptune-like planets, 74 gas giants, 36 super-Earths, 19 Earth-sized planets. They orbit 198 different stars; 25 of those stars have more than one planet in this list. The size classes are a rough sort by radius or mass; the archive records only what was measured, so planets without a measured size are listed as plain planets.

That is 3.6% of all 6,470 confirmed planets in the archive. The year before, 2019, had 194. Discovery counts rise and fall with the telescopes in operation and with the years in which large batches of candidates were confirmed together, so a single year says little about the pace of the field.
