Exoplanets discovered in 2011

141 new confirmed planets.

The archive lists 141 confirmed planets as discovered in 2011, which brought the total to 639. Most were found by the transit method (80 of them). Others used radial velocity (49), eclipse timing variations (5), orbital brightness modulation (2). The facilities credited with most of them were Kepler (54), W. M. Keck Observatory (22), La Silla Observatory (17), HATNet (11).

The nearest of them is HD 20794 d, 19.6 light years away; the most massive is Kepler-39 b with about 6,388 Earth masses.

What the newly found planets are like

Of the 141 planets, 135 have a measured radius, 141 a measured mass and 140 an orbital period in the archive. The median radius is 12.2 Earth radii (smallest 0.57, largest 22.2). The median orbit takes 16.3 days. The median distance is 793 light years and the farthest is 9,915. By the simple size classes used on this site they are: 94 gas giants, 33 Neptune-like planets, 7 Earth-sized planets, 7 super-Earths. They orbit 111 different stars; 21 of those stars have more than one planet in this list. The size classes are a rough sort by radius or mass; the archive records only what was measured, so planets without a measured size are listed as plain planets.

That is 2.2% of all 6,470 confirmed planets in the archive. The year before, 2010, had 93. Discovery counts rise and fall with the telescopes in operation and with the years in which large batches of candidates were confirmed together, so a single year says little about the pace of the field.
