Exoplanets discovered in 2009

87 new confirmed planets.

The archive lists 87 confirmed planets as discovered in 2009, which brought the total to 405. Most were found by the radial velocity method (66 of them). Others used transit (18), microlensing (2), eclipse timing variations (1). The facilities credited with most of them were La Silla Observatory (21), W. M. Keck Observatory (16), Lick Observatory (7), SuperWASP (7).

The nearest of them is GJ 581 e, 20.5 light years away; the most massive is HD 16760 b with about 6,833 Earth masses.

What the newly found planets are like

Of the 87 planets, 82 have a measured radius, 87 a measured mass and 85 an orbital period in the archive. The median radius is 13.2 Earth radii (smallest 1.38, largest 21.0). The median orbit takes 446 days. The median distance is 220 light years and the farthest is 25,114. By the simple size classes used on this site they are: 76 gas giants, 9 Neptune-like planets, 2 super-Earths. They orbit 75 different stars; 10 of those stars have more than one planet in this list. The size classes are a rough sort by radius or mass; the archive records only what was measured, so planets without a measured size are listed as plain planets.

That is 1.3% of all 6,470 confirmed planets in the archive. The year before, 2008, had 62. Discovery counts rise and fall with the telescopes in operation and with the years in which large batches of candidates were confirmed together, so a single year says little about the pace of the field.
