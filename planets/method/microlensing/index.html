Exoplanets found by microlensing: 294 planets

How the method works

When a star passes in front of a more distant star, gravity bends and brightens the light of the background star. A planet around the nearer star adds a short extra blip. It is a one-time event that cannot be repeated, but it can find planets far from their stars.

What it has found

The archive lists 294 confirmed planets discovered by microlensing, the first in 2004. The planets found this way that have a measured radius have a median radius of 12.6 Earth radii. The table shows up to 40 of them.

What the microlensing planets are like

Of the 294 planets, 294 have a measured radius, 294 a measured mass and 12 an orbital period in the archive. The median radius is 12.6 Earth radii (smallest 1.09, largest 14.3). The median orbit takes 3,142 days. The median distance is 20,515 light years and the farthest is 27,201. By the simple size classes used on this site they are: 219 gas giants, 59 Neptune-like planets, 12 super-Earths, 4 Earth-sized planets. They orbit 284 different stars; 10 of those stars have more than one planet in this list. The size classes are a rough sort by radius or mass; the archive records only what was measured, so planets without a measured size are listed as plain planets.

4.5% of all the confirmed planets in the archive were found this way. The busiest year was 2022, with 51 planets, and the facilities credited with most are KMTNet (149), OGLE (104), MOA (34). A method finds some kinds of planets better than others, so the mix of sizes and orbits above is a property of the method as well as of the planets.
