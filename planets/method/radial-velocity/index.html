Exoplanets found by radial velocity: 1,205 planets

How the method works

A planet pulls its star slightly, so the star wobbles toward and away from us. The wobble shifts the colours of the star's light by a tiny amount, which spectrographs can measure. It gives the planet's orbit and a minimum mass.

What it has found

The archive lists 1,205 confirmed planets discovered by radial velocity, the first in 1995. The planets found this way that have a measured radius have a median radius of 12.6 Earth radii. The table shows up to 40 of them.

What the radial velocity planets are like

Of the 1,205 planets, 1,168 have a measured radius, 1,204 a measured mass and 1,205 an orbital period in the archive. The median radius is 12.6 Earth radii (smallest 0.637, largest 15.6). The median orbit takes 306 days. The median distance is 145 light years and the farthest is 5,022. By the simple size classes used on this site they are: 848 gas giants, 261 Neptune-like planets, 63 super-Earths, 32 Earth-sized planets. They orbit 879 different stars; 231 of those stars have more than one planet in this list. The size classes are a rough sort by radius or mass; the archive records only what was measured, so planets without a measured size are listed as plain planets.

18.6% of all the confirmed planets in the archive were found this way. The busiest year was 2022, with 115 planets, and the facilities credited with most are La Silla Observatory (306), Multiple Observatories (298), W. M. Keck Observatory (182). A method finds some kinds of planets better than others, so the mix of sizes and orbits above is a property of the method as well as of the planets.
