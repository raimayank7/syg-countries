Planets discovered by Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS)

The NASA Exoplanet Archive credits Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) with the discovery of 1,026 confirmed planets, found between 2018 and 2026. The most used method was transit (1,022 planets). The table lists up to 40 of them, those with the most measured values first.

What the discovered planets are like

Of the 1,026 planets, 1,025 have a measured radius, 1,024 a measured mass and 1,026 an orbital period in the archive. The median radius is 3.10 Earth radii (smallest 0.566, largest 25.0). The median orbit takes 5.71 days. The median distance is 375 light years and the farthest is 3,942. By the simple size classes used on this site they are: 467 Neptune-like planets, 360 gas giants, 137 super-Earths, 62 Earth-sized planets. They orbit 869 different stars; 114 of those stars have more than one planet in this list. The size classes are a rough sort by radius or mass; the archive records only what was measured, so planets without a measured size are listed as plain planets.

The busiest year for this facility was 2026, with 287 planets credited. Its planets were found with transit (1,022), transit timing variations (4). A facility is credited in the archive for the discovery paper, which can name a telescope, a spacecraft or a survey team, so counts of one facility are not directly comparable with those of another kind of facility.
