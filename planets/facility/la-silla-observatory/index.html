Planets discovered by La Silla Observatory

The NASA Exoplanet Archive credits La Silla Observatory with the discovery of 311 confirmed planets, found between 1999 and 2026. The most used method was radial velocity (306 planets). The table lists up to 40 of them, those with the most measured values first.

What the discovered planets are like

Of the 311 planets, 291 have a measured radius, 311 a measured mass and 311 an orbital period in the archive. The median radius is 9.32 Earth radii (smallest 0.692, largest 14.3). The median orbit takes 61.3 days. The median distance is 117 light years and the farthest is 3,018. By the simple size classes used on this site they are: 174 gas giants, 96 Neptune-like planets, 24 super-Earths, 17 Earth-sized planets. They orbit 209 different stars; 64 of those stars have more than one planet in this list. The size classes are a rough sort by radius or mass; the archive records only what was measured, so planets without a measured size are listed as plain planets.

The busiest year for this facility was 2019, with 31 planets credited. Its planets were found with radial velocity (306), transit (5). A facility is credited in the archive for the discovery paper, which can name a telescope, a spacecraft or a survey team, so counts of one facility are not directly comparable with those of another kind of facility.
