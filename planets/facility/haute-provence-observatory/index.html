Planets discovered by Haute-Provence Observatory

The NASA Exoplanet Archive credits Haute-Provence Observatory with the discovery of 69 confirmed planets, found between 1995 and 2025. The most used method was radial velocity (69 planets). The table lists up to 40 of them, those with the most measured values first.

What the discovered planets are like

Of the 69 planets, 68 have a measured radius, 69 a measured mass and 69 an orbital period in the archive. The median radius is 13.1 Earth radii (smallest 1.28, largest 14.3). The median orbit takes 248 days. The median distance is 171 light years and the farthest is 803. By the simple size classes used on this site they are: 59 gas giants, 8 Neptune-like planets, 2 super-Earths. They orbit 59 different stars; 7 of those stars have more than one planet in this list. The size classes are a rough sort by radius or mass; the archive records only what was measured, so planets without a measured size are listed as plain planets.

The busiest year for this facility was 2021, with 12 planets credited. Its planets were found with radial velocity (69). A facility is credited in the archive for the discovery paper, which can name a telescope, a spacecraft or a survey team, so counts of one facility are not directly comparable with those of another kind of facility.
