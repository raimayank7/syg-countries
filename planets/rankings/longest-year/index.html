Exoplanets with the longest year

The planets with the longest orbital period in the archive.

The longest orbits take thousands of years. Such planets are mostly found by direct imaging, which needs a planet far enough from its star to be seen separately.

50 planets are listed. The values are the archive's preferred values, and planets flagged as controversial are left out. Open a planet for the full story.

How to read this list

6,055 of the 6,396 planets with preferred values in the archive have a measured orbital period, and only they can appear here. Across all of them the lowest value is 0.0907, the middle value 10.5 and the highest 402,000,000. The 50 planets in this list were found by radial velocity (32), imaging (18), in the years 2006 to 2026 (the middle year is 2022). A list like this shows what current methods can measure: the methods favour planets that are large, close to their stars or close to us, so the extremes depend on how planets are found as well as on what exists. Values are the archive's preferred ones and change as measurements improve.
