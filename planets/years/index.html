Exoplanets by year of discovery

The first confirmed planets orbiting an ordinary star were found in the 1990s. The pace jumped with the Kepler space telescope: the year 2016, with 1,504, is the busiest in the archive. Today the archive lists 6,470 confirmed planets. Pick a year to see which methods and telescopes found them.

How to use this page

The table shows how many confirmed exoplanets the archive credits to each year of discovery and the running total. The numbers rise in steps rather than steadily: a few planets a year in the 1990s, a jump when planets found by the Kepler space telescope were confirmed, and further jumps when large batches of candidates were confirmed together. A year is the year of the discovery paper in the archive, which can be later than the observations. Open a year to see the methods and facilities behind its planets and a list of them.
