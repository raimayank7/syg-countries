Arrow symbols to copy and paste: ← ↑ → ↓ ↔ ➜ ➡

Arrows are among the most copied characters on the web, and Unicode has hundreds of them. The plain ones, left, up, right, down and the double-headed and two-way forms, sit in the Arrows block; heavier, decorated and triangle-headed arrows are in Dingbats, the Supplemental Arrows blocks and Miscellaneous Symbols and Arrows. This list gathers every arrow in those blocks, in code order, so that you can find the shape you want and tap Copy.

A few things help. Arrows are ordinary text characters, so they take the colour and size of the text around them. Not every font draws all of them: a missing shape shows as an empty box, and the older Arrows block is covered by almost every font while the Supplemental blocks are not. Some arrows also have a picture (emoji) form, which phones draw in colour; the plain text form is the one without the variation selector.

This list has 553 characters from 7 Unicode blocks (Arrows, Dingbats, Miscellaneous Symbols and Arrows, Miscellaneous Technical, Supplemental Arrows-A, Supplemental Arrows-B and others). They were added between Unicode 1.1 and 17.0. Tap a symbol to copy it.

Common questions

How do I copy a symbol from this page?

Tap or click the symbol. It is copied to your clipboard, and a short message confirms it. Then paste it where you need it with Ctrl+V, or press and hold in a text field on a phone.

Why do some symbols look like empty boxes?

The font on your device does not have that character. The characters are valid Unicode, but each font covers only part of it. Try another app or font, or choose a more common symbol from the list.

Can I use these symbols in a username, a name or a file?

Many sites accept them, but some block symbols or turn them into question marks. Test on the site first. Avoid symbols in file names and passwords, where an unusual character can cause errors on another system.
