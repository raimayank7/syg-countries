Roman numeral characters to copy: Ⅰ Ⅱ Ⅲ Ⅳ Ⅴ Ⅹ

Roman numerals are normally written with the ordinary Latin letters I, V, X, L, C, D and M, and that is almost always the best choice. Unicode also has a block of Roman numeral characters (U+2160 to U+2188), including joined forms Ⅱ, Ⅲ, Ⅷ and Ⅻ, which were added for compatibility with East Asian character sets.

The joined forms look neater in vertical Asian text, but they are not read as the letters I and V by a search engine or a screen reader, so a page that must be found by the number "XII" should use plain letters. The list has both capital and small forms, and the symbols for 50, 100, 500 and 1,000 used in old texts.

This list has 40 characters from 1 Unicode block (Number Forms). They were added between Unicode 1.1 and 5.1. Tap a symbol to copy it.

Common questions

How do I copy a symbol from this page?

Tap or click the symbol. It is copied to your clipboard, and a short message confirms it. Then paste it where you need it with Ctrl+V, or press and hold in a text field on a phone.

Why do some symbols look like empty boxes?

The font on your device does not have that character. The characters are valid Unicode, but each font covers only part of it. Try another app or font, or choose a more common symbol from the list.

Can I use these symbols in a username, a name or a file?

Many sites accept them, but some block symbols or turn them into question marks. Test on the site first. Avoid symbols in file names and passwords, where an unusual character can cause errors on another system.
