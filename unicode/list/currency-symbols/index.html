Currency symbols to copy: ₹ $ € £ ¥ ₿

This list has every currency sign in Unicode, from the dollar and pound to the Indian rupee sign ₹ (U+20B9, added in Unicode 6.0), the euro €, the bitcoin sign ₿ and signs of money that few people use today. Most newer signs are in the Currency Symbols block (U+20A0 to U+20CF); the oldest, such as $, ¢, £, ¤ and ¥, were kept where they were in ASCII and Latin-1.

Copying beats typing for the rare ones. Two cautions: the same sign can stand for several currencies (the dollar sign is used by the US, Canada, Australia and others, and the yen and yuan share ¥), and whether the sign goes before or after the amount follows local custom. Also, if a person needs to read your page in an old program, the rupee sign needs a font that has been updated since 2010.

This list has 67 characters from 18 Unicode blocks (Arabic, Arabic Presentation Forms-A, Armenian, Basic Latin, Bengali, Common Indic Number Forms and others). They were added between Unicode 1.1 and 18.0. Tap a symbol to copy it.

Common questions

How do I copy a symbol from this page?

Tap or click the symbol. It is copied to your clipboard, and a short message confirms it. Then paste it where you need it with Ctrl+V, or press and hold in a text field on a phone.

Why do some symbols look like empty boxes?

The font on your device does not have that character. The characters are valid Unicode, but each font covers only part of it. Try another app or font, or choose a more common symbol from the list.

Can I use these symbols in a username, a name or a file?

Many sites accept them, but some block symbols or turn them into question marks. Test on the site first. Avoid symbols in file names and passwords, where an unusual character can cause errors on another system.
