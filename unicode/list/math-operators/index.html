Math symbols to copy: ± × ÷ ≠ ≤ ≥ ∞ √ ∑ ∫ ≈

The Mathematical Operators block (U+2200 to U+22FF) holds the signs used in algebra, logic and set theory: ∀ ∃ ∈ ⊂ ∪ ∩, the integral and sum signs, roots, comparisons and approximations. The most common everyday signs, such as ±, × and ÷, come from the Latin-1 block, and the plus sign and equals sign are ordinary ASCII.

For the minus sign use U+2212 and not the hyphen: it is as wide as the plus sign and keeps a minus from breaking at the end of a line. The multiplication sign × (U+00D7) is not the letter x. Fonts used for maths have full coverage; plain text fonts may draw some of the rarer operators as empty boxes.

This list has 266 characters from 3 Unicode blocks (Basic Latin, Latin-1 Supplement, Mathematical Operators). They were added between Unicode 1.1 and 3.2. Tap a symbol to copy it.

Common questions

How do I copy a symbol from this page?

Tap or click the symbol. It is copied to your clipboard, and a short message confirms it. Then paste it where you need it with Ctrl+V, or press and hold in a text field on a phone.

Why do some symbols look like empty boxes?

The font on your device does not have that character. The characters are valid Unicode, but each font covers only part of it. Try another app or font, or choose a more common symbol from the list.

Can I use these symbols in a username, a name or a file?

Many sites accept them, but some block symbols or turn them into question marks. Test on the site first. Avoid symbols in file names and passwords, where an unusual character can cause errors on another system.
