Quotation marks to copy: “ ” ‘ ’ „ « » ‹ ›

The typewriter quotes " and ' are the plain ASCII characters. Typeset text uses curly (typographic) quotes: “ ” for double quotes and ‘ ’ for single quotes, with different opening and closing shapes. The same signs are used differently in other languages: German opens with „ and closes with “; French uses « and »; Swedish and Finnish use ” on both sides.

The apostrophe in "don't" is the same character as the closing single quote ’ (U+2019), not the straight one. A common mistake in programs and code is pasting curly quotes into code, where only the straight quotes are valid. The list holds every character Unicode classes as an opening quote (Pi), a closing quote (Pf), or has "quotation mark" in its name.

This list has 42 characters from 9 Unicode blocks (Basic Latin, CJK Symbols and Punctuation, Dingbats, General Punctuation, Halfwidth and Fullwidth Forms, Latin-1 Supplement and others). They were added between Unicode 1.1 and 7.0. Tap a symbol to copy it.

Common questions

How do I copy a symbol from this page?

Tap or click the symbol. It is copied to your clipboard, and a short message confirms it. Then paste it where you need it with Ctrl+V, or press and hold in a text field on a phone.

Why do some symbols look like empty boxes?

The font on your device does not have that character. The characters are valid Unicode, but each font covers only part of it. Try another app or font, or choose a more common symbol from the list.

Can I use these symbols in a username, a name or a file?

Many sites accept them, but some block symbols or turn them into question marks. Test on the site first. Avoid symbols in file names and passwords, where an unusual character can cause errors on another system.
