Circled numbers to copy: ① ② ③ ⑩ ⑳ ⓿

Circled and parenthesised numbers are used for numbered steps, footnotes and ranking in places where you can only paste plain text. Unicode has circled numbers 1 to 20 in the Enclosed Alphanumerics block, further circled numbers up to 50 in CJK blocks, and several styles of "negative" (white on black) circled numbers.

Choose the style that fits: plain circles for a light look, dingbat circles for a heavier one. Remember that they are single characters; two-digit numbers other than those listed cannot be made by joining digits. Screen readers say each one by its name, such as "circled digit one", so use them for decoration of a list and not as the only way to number important steps.

This list has 132 characters from 4 Unicode blocks (Dingbats, Enclosed Alphanumeric Supplement, Enclosed Alphanumerics, Enclosed CJK Letters and Months). They were added between Unicode 1.1 and 7.0. Tap a symbol to copy it.

Common questions

How do I copy a symbol from this page?

Tap or click the symbol. It is copied to your clipboard, and a short message confirms it. Then paste it where you need it with Ctrl+V, or press and hold in a text field on a phone.

Why do some symbols look like empty boxes?

The font on your device does not have that character. The characters are valid Unicode, but each font covers only part of it. Try another app or font, or choose a more common symbol from the list.

Can I use these symbols in a username, a name or a file?

Many sites accept them, but some block symbols or turn them into question marks. Test on the site first. Avoid symbols in file names and passwords, where an unusual character can cause errors on another system.
