Superscript and subscript characters to copy: x² H₂O ⁿ ₙ

Superscript digits and signs (like ², ³, ⁴ and ⁺) and subscript digits (₀ to ₉, as in H₂O) are real characters in Unicode, so they stay raised or lowered when you paste them into plain text, a chat or a username, where you cannot use formatting.

The set is not complete. Superscript digits and a handful of letters (such as ⁿ and ⁱ) exist, and there are subscript digits and a limited set of subscript letters. If you need an arbitrary raised or lowered letter you may have to use formatting in your program instead. The characters ¹ ² ³ come from the Latin-1 block while the rest are in the Superscripts and Subscripts block (U+2070 to U+209F). Fonts draw them very small, so keep them for short expressions like units and chemical formulas.

This list has 94 characters from 9 Unicode blocks (Arabic Presentation Forms-A, Cyrillic Extended-D, Latin Extended-C, Latin Extended-F, Latin Extended-G, Latin-1 Supplement and others). They were added between Unicode 1.1 and 18.0. Tap a symbol to copy it.

Common questions

How do I copy a symbol from this page?

Tap or click the symbol. It is copied to your clipboard, and a short message confirms it. Then paste it where you need it with Ctrl+V, or press and hold in a text field on a phone.

Why do some symbols look like empty boxes?

The font on your device does not have that character. The characters are valid Unicode, but each font covers only part of it. Try another app or font, or choose a more common symbol from the list.

Can I use these symbols in a username, a name or a file?

Many sites accept them, but some block symbols or turn them into question marks. Test on the site first. Avoid symbols in file names and passwords, where an unusual character can cause errors on another system.
