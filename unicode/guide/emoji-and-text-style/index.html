How emoji work in Unicode

An emoji looks like a single picture, but under the hood it is text: one Unicode character or a short sequence of them. Knowing the parts explains many odd things, such as why the same symbol looks different on two phones or why an emoji shows as a black-and-white symbol in one app and in colour in another.

Text style and picture style

Some characters can be drawn as a plain text symbol or as a coloured emoji picture, for example the heart ❤ or the check mark ✔. Two invisible characters ask for one or the other: U+FE0E (variation selector 15) asks for text style and U+FE0F (variation selector 16) asks for picture style. Most apps follow the request, though not all. Characters in the picture block, like 😀, are drawn as emoji unless you ask otherwise. Every character page on this site that has an emoji form offers both versions to copy.

Skin tones

The five skin tone modifiers are the characters U+1F3FB to U+1F3FF. Put one after a person or hand emoji, and the emoji is drawn in that tone: 👍 followed by the modifier is one picture, but two characters. A program that counts characters will count two.

Flags

A country flag is not one character. It is two regional indicator letters, the two-letter country code written with special letters. The flag of India is the pair for I and N: 🇮🇳 (U+1F1EE + U+1F1F3). If the pair is not a valid country code, or the device has no flag for it, you see the two letters instead. On some Windows systems flags always show as letters; this is a choice of the platform, not a fault of the text.

Joined emoji

Many emoji are a sequence of other emoji joined by the zero width joiner U+200D. For example, the woman technologist is woman + joiner + laptop: 👩‍💻 (U+1F469 U+200D U+1F4BB). Families, couples, professions, and hair variants are made this way. A device that does not know the combined emoji shows the separate pictures one after another. A string of such an emoji has several code points, a length greater than one, and cannot be cut in the middle without breaking it.

Keycaps and tags

The keycap digits are a plain digit or sign, then U+FE0F, then the combining enclosing keycap U+20E3. Some flags of regions, such as England or Scotland, are a black flag followed by a hidden sequence of tag characters that spell the region code.

Why they differ between phones

Unicode and the emoji committee decide the meaning and the name of each emoji, but not its picture: Apple, Google, Samsung, Microsoft and others each draw their own. This is why an emoji can look friendly on one phone and sarcastic on another. If it matters, check how it looks on the common platforms.
