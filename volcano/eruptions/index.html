Significant volcanic events

The NOAA database lists 903 significant events (828 of them eruptions) from 4360 BC to 2026. Deaths are estimates; for older events they are rounded or disputed, and some records combine the effects of an eruption with a tsunami or earthquake. The first table is the 30 events with the most recorded deaths; the second is every event, with a filter.

746 of the events have a Volcanic Explosivity Index and 480 have an estimated death toll, which adds up to 335,760 deaths over the whole record; one very large event can dominate such a total, so look at single events and not at the sum. Since the year 1000 the database lists 852 events; the busiest centuries are the 1900s (378), the 2000s (152), the 1800s (150). A rise in recent centuries may partly reflect better records and larger populations near volcanoes, so it should not be read as a change in how often volcanoes erupt. Several events can belong to one volcano and one eruption sequence, so the number of events is not the number of separate eruptions.
