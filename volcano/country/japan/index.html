Volcanoes of Japan

113 volcanoes

NOAA's Volcano Location Database lists 113 volcanoes in Japan: 51 stratovolcano, 19 submarine volcano, 13 caldera, 8 complex volcano, 7 shield volcano, 7 lava dome.

The highest is Fujisan at 3,776 m (12,388 ft).

The significant-eruption database has 148 events for volcanoes of Japan, with an estimated 22,680 deaths in all.

Japan in the database as a whole

With 113 volcanoes Japan is number 4 of 95 countries and territories in the NOAA location database, and holds 7.0% of the 1,609 volcanoes listed worldwide. Their latitudes run from 21.3° to 45.2°, so they lie in the northern hemisphere. Of the 113 volcanoes with a recorded height the median is 1,011 m; 56 are below 1,000 m, 54 are between 1,000 and 2,999 m and 3 are 3,000 m or higher. By status in the database: 64 Historical, 16 Holocene, 11 Radiocarbon, 7 Tephrochronology.

40 of the volcanoes of Japan (35%) have at least one significant event in the NOAA eruption database, and the ones with most events are Asamayama (18), Asosan (16), Aira (15). The database records only significant events, so the share says how many volcanoes have caused deaths or damage or were notably large, and says nothing about how many are active today.

The events of this country run from 4350 BC to 2023, and the one with the most recorded deaths is that of 21 May 1792 (Unzendake, an estimated 15,000 deaths).
