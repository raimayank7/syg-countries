Volcanoes of United States

191 volcanoes

NOAA's Volcano Location Database lists 191 volcanoes in United States: 87 stratovolcano, 23 shield volcano, 19 volcanic field, 17 submarine volcano, 17 cinder cone, 6 lava dome.

The highest is Churchill at 5,005 m (16,421 ft).

The significant-eruption database has 50 events for volcanoes of United States, with an estimated 5,568 deaths in all.

United States in the database as a whole

With 191 volcanoes United States is number 1 of 95 countries and territories in the NOAA location database, and holds 11.9% of the 1,609 volcanoes listed worldwide. Their latitudes run from -14.6° to 66.3°, so they lie in the northern and southern hemispheres. Of the 189 volcanoes with a recorded height the median is 1,478 m; 61 are below 1,000 m, 106 are between 1,000 and 2,999 m and 22 are 3,000 m or higher. By status in the database: 63 Historical, 58 Holocene, 27 Radiocarbon, 12 Uncertain.

23 of the volcanoes of United States (12%) have at least one significant event in the NOAA eruption database, and the ones with most events are Kilauea (16), Augustine (3), St. Helens (3). The database records only significant events, so the share says how many volcanoes have caused deaths or damage or were notably large, and says nothing about how many are active today.

The events of this country run from 1900 BC to 2018, and the one with the most recorded deaths is that of November 1790 (Kilauea, an estimated 5,405 deaths).
