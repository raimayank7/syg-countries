European Robin (Erithacus rubecula)

Aves, family Muscicapidae.

Erithacus rubecula (Linnaeus, 1758) is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called European Robin in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Muscicapidae, order Passeriformes, class Aves, kingdom Animalia.

It was first published in Syst. Nat. ed. 10 p. 188. The name was first used in the form Motacilla rubecula Linnaeus, 1758; the species has since been placed in the genus Erithacus.

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 11,551,193 occurrence records of Erithacus rubecula, from 1792 to 2026. The year with most records is 2025 (1,034,930 records). The records are of these kinds: human observation (11,517,363), machine observation (camera, sensor, recording) (13,883), preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (9,337), living specimen (zoo or garden) (4,859), material sample (2,714).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (11,480,776), before 1900: 798 (0 %); 1900 to 1949: 6,722 (0 %); 1950 to 1999: 1,078,818 (9 %); 2000 to 2019: 5,001,699 (44 %); 2020 and later: 5,392,739 (47 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 100 % of all records are human observations.

Common questions

What is the scientific name of European Robin?

The accepted scientific name is Erithacus rubecula (Linnaeus, 1758).

What family does European Robin belong to?

Erithacus rubecula belongs to the family Muscicapidae, in the order Passeriformes.

Where is European Robin recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is United Kingdom (1,843,270 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
