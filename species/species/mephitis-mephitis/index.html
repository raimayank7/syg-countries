Striped Skunk (Mephitis mephitis)

Mammalia, family Mephitidae.

Mephitis mephitis (Schreber, 1776) is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called striped skunk in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Mephitidae, order Carnivora, class Mammalia, kingdom Animalia.

It was first published in Die Säugethiere vol.3 17 p.pl. 121[1776], see also text, 3(26):444, 588 (index)[1777]. The name was first used in the form Viverra mephitis Schreber, 1776; the species has since been placed in the genus Mephitis.

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 46,526 occurrence records of Mephitis mephitis, from 1839 to 2026. The year with most records is 2026 (5,954 records). The records are of these kinds: human observation (35,813), preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (9,832), fossil specimen (431), literature record (301), machine observation (camera, sensor, recording) (63).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (45,082), before 1900: 1,137 (3 %); 1900 to 1949: 3,517 (8 %); 1950 to 1999: 2,963 (7 %); 2000 to 2019: 7,252 (16 %); 2020 and later: 30,213 (67 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 77 % of all records are human observations.

Common questions

What is the scientific name of striped skunk?

The accepted scientific name is Mephitis mephitis (Schreber, 1776).

What family does striped skunk belong to?

Mephitis mephitis belongs to the family Mephitidae, in the order Carnivora.

Where is striped skunk recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is United States (38,162 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
