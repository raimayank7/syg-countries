Giant Anteater (Myrmecophaga tridactyla)

Mammalia, family Myrmecophagidae.

Myrmecophaga tridactyla Linnaeus, 1758 is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called giant anteater in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Myrmecophagidae, order Pilosa, class Mammalia, kingdom Animalia.

It was first published in Syst. Nat., 10th ed. vol.1 p.35.

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 12,134 occurrence records of Myrmecophaga tridactyla, from 1816 to 2026. The year with most records is 2021 (1,900 records). The records are of these kinds: machine observation (camera, sensor, recording) (6,215), human observation (4,821), preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (919), material sample (92), unspecified occurrence (71).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (11,361), before 1900: 36 (0 %); 1900 to 1949: 177 (2 %); 1950 to 1999: 184 (2 %); 2000 to 2019: 2,897 (25 %); 2020 and later: 8,067 (71 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 40 % of all records are human observations.

Common questions

What is the scientific name of giant anteater?

The accepted scientific name is Myrmecophaga tridactyla Linnaeus, 1758.

What family does giant anteater belong to?

Myrmecophaga tridactyla belongs to the family Myrmecophagidae, in the order Pilosa.

Where is giant anteater recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is Colombia (7,457 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
