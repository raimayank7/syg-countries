Okapi (Okapia johnstoni)

Mammalia, family Giraffidae.

Okapia johnstoni (P.L.Sclater, 1901) is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called Okapi in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Giraffidae, order Artiodactyla, class Mammalia, kingdom Animalia.

It was first published in Proc. Zool. Soc. Lond. vol.1901 1 p.50. The name was first used in the form Equus johnstoni P.L.Sclater, 1901; the species has since been placed in the genus Okapia.

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 317 occurrence records of Okapia johnstoni, from 1903 to 2023. The year with most records is 1910 (14 records). The records are of these kinds: preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (300), material sample (8), human observation (5), literature record (3), observation (1).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (170), 1900 to 1949: 76 (45 %); 1950 to 1999: 61 (36 %); 2000 to 2019: 30 (18 %); 2020 and later: 3 (2 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 2 % of all records are human observations.

Relatives in this list

Other species of the family Giraffidae: Giraffa camelopardalis (giraffe).

Common questions

What is the scientific name of Okapi?

The accepted scientific name is Okapia johnstoni (P.L.Sclater, 1901).

What family does Okapi belong to?

Okapia johnstoni belongs to the family Giraffidae, in the order Artiodactyla.

Where is Okapi recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is The Democratic Republic Of Congo (96 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
