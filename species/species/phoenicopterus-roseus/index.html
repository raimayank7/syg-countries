Greater Flamingo (Phoenicopterus roseus)

Aves, family Phoenicopteridae.

Phoenicopterus roseus Pallas, 1811 is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called Greater Flamingo in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Phoenicopteridae, order Phoenicopteriformes, class Aves, kingdom Animalia.

It was first published in Pallas, P. S. (1811). Zoographia Rosso-Asiatica, Sistens Omnium Animalium in Extenso Imperio Rossico Et Adjacentibus Maribus Observatorum Recensionem, Domicilia, Mores Et Desciptiones, Anatomen Atque Icones Plurimorum, Vol. 2..

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 548,675 occurrence records of Phoenicopterus roseus, from 1832 to 2026. The year with most records is 2025 (67,891 records). The records are of these kinds: human observation (547,155), preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (1,267), machine observation (camera, sensor, recording) (90), literature record (85), unspecified occurrence (39).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (545,179), before 1900: 94 (0 %); 1900 to 1949: 390 (0 %); 1950 to 1999: 23,734 (4 %); 2000 to 2019: 210,549 (39 %); 2020 and later: 310,412 (57 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 100 % of all records are human observations.

Common questions

What is the scientific name of Greater Flamingo?

The accepted scientific name is Phoenicopterus roseus Pallas, 1811.

What family does Greater Flamingo belong to?

Phoenicopterus roseus belongs to the family Phoenicopteridae, in the order Phoenicopteriformes.

Where is Greater Flamingo recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is Spain (150,775 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
