Vervet Monkey (Chlorocebus pygerythrus)

Mammalia, family Cercopithecidae.

Chlorocebus pygerythrus (F.Cuvier, 1821) is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called vervet monkey in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Cercopithecidae, order Primates, class Mammalia, kingdom Animalia.

It was first published in Hist. Nat. Mamm. vol.24 p.2.

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 15,773 occurrence records of Chlorocebus pygerythrus, from 1820 to 2026. The year with most records is 2026 (1,850 records). The records are of these kinds: human observation (13,480), preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (1,332), machine observation (camera, sensor, recording) (921), literature record (28), unspecified occurrence (8).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (15,296), before 1900: 22 (0 %); 1900 to 1949: 373 (2 %); 1950 to 1999: 775 (5 %); 2000 to 2019: 5,752 (38 %); 2020 and later: 8,374 (55 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 85 % of all records are human observations.

Relatives in this list

Other species of the family Cercopithecidae: Macaca mulatta (rhesus monkey), Macaca sylvanus (Barbary macaque), Papio anubis (olive baboon).

Common questions

What is the scientific name of vervet monkey?

The accepted scientific name is Chlorocebus pygerythrus (F.Cuvier, 1821).

What family does vervet monkey belong to?

Chlorocebus pygerythrus belongs to the family Cercopithecidae, in the order Primates.

Where is vervet monkey recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is South Africa (7,656 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
