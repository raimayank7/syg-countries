White Oak (Quercus alba)

Magnoliopsida, family Fagaceae.

Quercus alba L. is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called White Oak in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Fagaceae, order Fagales, class Magnoliopsida, kingdom Plantae.

It was first published in Sp. Pl.: 996 (1753).

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 58,892 occurrence records of Quercus alba, from 1714 to 2026. The year with most records is 2025 (8,993 records). The records are of these kinds: human observation (44,124), preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (14,384), unspecified occurrence (281), living specimen (zoo or garden) (55), material sample (23).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (54,838), before 1900: 675 (1 %); 1900 to 1949: 3,361 (6 %); 1950 to 1999: 5,186 (9 %); 2000 to 2019: 10,796 (20 %); 2020 and later: 34,820 (63 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 75 % of all records are human observations.

Relatives in this list

Common questions

What is the scientific name of White Oak?

The accepted scientific name is Quercus alba L..

What family does White Oak belong to?

Quercus alba belongs to the family Fagaceae, in the order Fagales.

Where is White Oak recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is United States (54,473 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
