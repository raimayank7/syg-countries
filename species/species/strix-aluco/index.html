Tawny Owl (Strix aluco)

Aves, family Strigidae.

Strix aluco Linnaeus, 1758 is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called Tawny Owl in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Strigidae, order Strigiformes, class Aves, kingdom Animalia.

It was first published in Linnaeus, & Carolus. (1758). Systema Naturae Per Regna Tria Naturae, Secundum Classes, Ordines, Genera, Species, Cum Characteribus, Differentiis, Synonymis, Locis, 10th Edition, Vol. 1..

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 1,052,563 occurrence records of Strix aluco, from 1575 to 2026. The year with most records is 2025 (73,856 records). The records are of these kinds: human observation (1,042,371), preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (7,059), machine observation (camera, sensor, recording) (2,165), material sample (408), literature record (343).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (1,018,974), before 1900: 482 (0 %); 1900 to 1949: 2,192 (0 %); 1950 to 1999: 118,685 (12 %); 2000 to 2019: 489,716 (48 %); 2020 and later: 407,899 (40 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 99 % of all records are human observations.

Relatives in this list

Other species of the family Strigidae: Athene noctua (Little Owl), Bubo bubo (Eurasian Eagle-Owl).

Common questions

What is the scientific name of Tawny Owl?

The accepted scientific name is Strix aluco Linnaeus, 1758.

What family does Tawny Owl belong to?

Strix aluco belongs to the family Strigidae, in the order Strigiformes.

Where is Tawny Owl recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is Sweden (305,219 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
