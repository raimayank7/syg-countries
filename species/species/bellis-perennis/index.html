Daisy (Bellis perennis)

Magnoliopsida, family Asteraceae.

Bellis perennis L. is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called Daisy in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Asteraceae, order Asterales, class Magnoliopsida, kingdom Plantae.

It was first published in L. (1753). In: Sp. Pl. 886..

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 1,113,810 occurrence records of Bellis perennis, from 1500 to 2026. The year with most records is 2025 (51,385 records). The records are of these kinds: human observation (1,077,670), observation (14,811), preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (13,503), literature record (5,244), unspecified occurrence (1,776).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (951,364), before 1900: 1,903 (0 %); 1900 to 1949: 25,467 (3 %); 1950 to 1999: 213,566 (22 %); 2000 to 2019: 423,000 (44 %); 2020 and later: 287,428 (30 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 97 % of all records are human observations.

Relatives in this list

Other species of the family Asteraceae: Helianthus annuus (Sunflower), Taraxacum officinale (Dandelion).

Common questions

What is the scientific name of Daisy?

The accepted scientific name is Bellis perennis L..

What family does Daisy belong to?

Bellis perennis belongs to the family Asteraceae, in the order Asterales.

Where is Daisy recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is United Kingdom (403,285 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
