Sugar Maple (Acer saccharum)

Magnoliopsida, family Sapindaceae.

Acer saccharum Marshall is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called sugar maple in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Sapindaceae, order Sapindales, class Magnoliopsida, kingdom Plantae.

It was first published in Marshall. (1785). In: Arbust 4..

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 69,802 occurrence records of Acer saccharum, from 1748 to 2026. The year with most records is 1995 (15,460 records). The records are of these kinds: human observation (56,234), preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (12,402), unspecified occurrence (614), living specimen (zoo or garden) (480), material sample (48).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (66,661), before 1900: 759 (1 %); 1900 to 1949: 3,049 (5 %); 1950 to 1999: 20,638 (31 %); 2000 to 2019: 15,372 (23 %); 2020 and later: 26,843 (40 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 81 % of all records are human observations.

Common questions

What is the scientific name of sugar maple?

The accepted scientific name is Acer saccharum Marshall.

What family does sugar maple belong to?

Acer saccharum belongs to the family Sapindaceae, in the order Sapindales.

Where is sugar maple recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is United States (55,138 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
