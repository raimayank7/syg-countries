Maize (Zea mays)

Liliopsida, family Poaceae.

Zea mays L. is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called Maize in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Poaceae, order Poales, class Liliopsida, kingdom Plantae.

It was first published in Sp. Pl.: 971 (1753).

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 328,635 occurrence records of Zea mays, from 1674 to 2026. The year with most records is 2022 (5,700 records). The records are of these kinds: unspecified occurrence (156,810), preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (83,165), living specimen (zoo or garden) (62,789), human observation (24,600), material sample (639).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (112,929), before 1900: 412 (0 %); 1900 to 1949: 8,257 (7 %); 1950 to 1999: 63,699 (56 %); 2000 to 2019: 26,958 (24 %); 2020 and later: 13,603 (12 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 7 % of all records are human observations.

Relatives in this list

Common questions

What is the scientific name of Maize?

The accepted scientific name is Zea mays L..

What family does Maize belong to?

Zea mays belongs to the family Poaceae, in the order Poales.

Where is Maize recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is Mexico (69,157 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
