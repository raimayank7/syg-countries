Asian Elephant (Elephas maximus)

Mammalia, family Elephantidae.

Elephas maximus Linnaeus, 1758 is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called Asian elephant in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Elephantidae, order Proboscidea, class Mammalia, kingdom Animalia.

It was first published in Syst. Nat., 10th ed. vol.1 p.33.

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 13,485 occurrence records of Elephas maximus, from 1783 to 2026. The year with most records is 2025 (1,313 records). The records are of these kinds: human observation (7,028), preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (5,414), machine observation (camera, sensor, recording) (394), material sample (243), fossil specimen (195).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (12,344), before 1900: 71 (1 %); 1900 to 1949: 68 (1 %); 1950 to 1999: 107 (1 %); 2000 to 2019: 6,799 (55 %); 2020 and later: 5,299 (43 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 52 % of all records are human observations.

Relatives in this list

Other species of the genus Elephas: Elephas maximus indicus (Bengal Elephant). Other species of the family Elephantidae: Elephas maximus indicus (Bengal Elephant), Loxodonta africana (African elephant).

Common questions

What is the scientific name of Asian elephant?

The accepted scientific name is Elephas maximus Linnaeus, 1758.

What family does Asian elephant belong to?

Elephas maximus belongs to the family Elephantidae, in the order Proboscidea.

Where is Asian elephant recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is United States (4,487 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
