German Cockroach (Blattella germanica)

Insecta, family Ectobiidae.

Blattella germanica (Linnaeus, 1767) is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called German Cockroach in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Ectobiidae, order Blattodea, class Insecta, kingdom Animalia.

It was first published in Linnaeus. 1767. Systema naturae 1, ed. 12, Holmiae. The name was first used in the form Blatta germanica Linnaeus, 1767; the species has since been placed in the genus Blattella.

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 8,559 occurrence records of Blattella germanica, from 1869 to 2026. The year with most records is 2025 (1,257 records). The records are of these kinds: human observation (5,814), preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (1,894), material sample (814), unspecified occurrence (16), machine observation (camera, sensor, recording) (9).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (7,651), before 1900: 18 (0 %); 1900 to 1949: 335 (4 %); 1950 to 1999: 953 (12 %); 2000 to 2019: 1,157 (15 %); 2020 and later: 5,188 (68 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 68 % of all records are human observations.

Common questions

What is the scientific name of German Cockroach?

The accepted scientific name is Blattella germanica (Linnaeus, 1767).

What family does German Cockroach belong to?

Blattella germanica belongs to the family Ectobiidae, in the order Blattodea.

Where is German Cockroach recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is United States (2,452 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
