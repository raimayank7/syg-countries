Atlantic Salmon (Salmo salar)

Animalia, family Salmonidae.

Salmo salar Linnaeus, 1758 is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called Atlantic Salmon in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Salmonidae, order Salmoniformes, class not assigned, kingdom Animalia.

It was first published in Linnaeus, C. (1758). Systema Naturae per regna tria naturae, secundum classes, ordines, genera, species, cum characteribus, differentiis, synonymis, locis. <em>Editio decima, reformata [10th revised edition], vol. 1: 824 pp. Laurentius Salvius: Holmiae.</em>.

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 1,515,493 occurrence records of Salmo salar, from 1551 to 2026. The year with most records is 2021 (134,550 records). The records are of these kinds: human observation (882,393), machine observation (camera, sensor, recording) (378,375), material sample (207,081), preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (31,335), unspecified occurrence (16,285).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (1,489,090), before 1900: 8,828 (1 %); 1900 to 1949: 15,032 (1 %); 1950 to 1999: 321,586 (22 %); 2000 to 2019: 734,720 (49 %); 2020 and later: 408,924 (27 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 58 % of all records are human observations.

Relatives in this list

Common questions

What is the scientific name of Atlantic Salmon?

The accepted scientific name is Salmo salar Linnaeus, 1758.

What family does Atlantic Salmon belong to?

Salmo salar belongs to the family Salmonidae, in the order Salmoniformes.

Where is Atlantic Salmon recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is France (881,661 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
