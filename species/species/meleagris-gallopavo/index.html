Wild Turkey (Meleagris gallopavo)

Aves, family Phasianidae.

Meleagris gallopavo Linnaeus, 1758 is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called Wild Turkey in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Phasianidae, order Galliformes, class Aves, kingdom Animalia.

It was first published in Linnaeus, Carolus (1758): Systema Naturae per regna tria naturae: secundum classes, ordines, genera, species, cum characteribus, differentiis, synonymis, locis. Stockholm: Laurentius Salvius, DOI: https://doi.org/10.5962/bhl.title.542, URL: https://archive.org/download/mobot31753000798865/mobot31753000798865.pdf.

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 2,759,908 occurrence records of Meleagris gallopavo, from 1800 to 2026. The year with most records is 2025 (332,539 records). The records are of these kinds: human observation (2,733,864), observation (20,460), preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (3,874), material sample (717), fossil specimen (652).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (2,755,724), before 1900: 386 (0 %); 1900 to 1949: 1,151 (0 %); 1950 to 1999: 25,657 (1 %); 2000 to 2019: 1,044,147 (38 %); 2020 and later: 1,684,383 (61 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 99 % of all records are human observations.

Relatives in this list

Other species of the family Phasianidae: Gallus gallus (Red Junglefowl), Pavo cristatus (Indian Peafowl).

Common questions

What is the scientific name of Wild Turkey?

The accepted scientific name is Meleagris gallopavo Linnaeus, 1758.

What family does Wild Turkey belong to?

Meleagris gallopavo belongs to the family Phasianidae, in the order Galliformes.

Where is Wild Turkey recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is United States (2,451,177 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
