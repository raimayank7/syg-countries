Moose (Alces alces)

Mammalia, family Cervidae.

Alces alces (Linnaeus, 1758) is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called moose in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Cervidae, order Artiodactyla, class Mammalia, kingdom Animalia.

It was first published in Syst. Nat., 10th ed. vol.1 p.66. The name was first used in the form Cervus alces Linnaeus, 1758; the species has since been placed in the genus Alces.

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 151,214 occurrence records of Alces alces, from 1800 to 2026. The year with most records is 2018 (13,445 records). The records are of these kinds: human observation (143,662), preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (4,179), literature record (1,701), fossil specimen (603), machine observation (camera, sensor, recording) (537).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (148,323), before 1900: 143 (0 %); 1900 to 1949: 658 (0 %); 1950 to 1999: 13,109 (9 %); 2000 to 2019: 70,188 (47 %); 2020 and later: 64,225 (43 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 95 % of all records are human observations.

Relatives in this list

Other species of the family Cervidae: Axis axis (chital), Cervus elaphus (red deer), Cervus nippon (Sika Deer), Odocoileus virginianus (white-tailed deer), Rangifer tarandus (reindeer).

Common questions

What is the scientific name of moose?

The accepted scientific name is Alces alces (Linnaeus, 1758).

What family does moose belong to?

Alces alces belongs to the family Cervidae, in the order Artiodactyla.

Where is moose recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is Estonia (34,493 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
