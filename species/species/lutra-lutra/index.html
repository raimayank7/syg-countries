European Otter (Lutra lutra)

Mammalia, family Mustelidae.

Lutra lutra (Linnaeus, 1758) is the accepted scientific name of the species commonly called European otter in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. It is a species in the family Mustelidae, order Carnivora, class Mammalia, kingdom Animalia.

It was first published in Syst. Nat., 10th ed. vol.1 p.45. The name was first used in the form Mustela lutra Linnaeus, 1758; the species has since been placed in the genus Lutra.

Where it has been recorded

GBIF.org holds 326,483 occurrence records of Lutra lutra, from 1557 to 2026. The year with most records is 2014 (25,780 records). The records are of these kinds: human observation (303,384), preserved specimen (museum or herbarium) (16,332), literature record (3,101), machine observation (camera, sensor, recording) (2,692), unspecified occurrence (677).

Records through time

Of the records that carry a year (302,719), before 1900: 902 (0 %); 1900 to 1949: 1,833 (1 %); 1950 to 1999: 39,773 (13 %); 2000 to 2019: 180,305 (60 %); 2020 and later: 79,906 (26 %). Recording has grown fast in recent decades, mostly because of photo sharing and citizen-science apps, so a rise in records does not mean a rise in numbers of the species. About 93 % of all records are human observations.

Common questions

What is the scientific name of European otter?

The accepted scientific name is Lutra lutra (Linnaeus, 1758).

What family does European otter belong to?

Lutra lutra belongs to the family Mustelidae, in the order Carnivora.

Where is European otter recorded most often?

In the GBIF records the country with the most is United Kingdom (89,990 records). This reflects where the species is recorded, which depends on how many observers there are.
