Family Cupressaceae: 2 species in this list

The family Cupressaceae belongs to the order Pinales, class Pinopsida. This site lists 2 of its species: Sequoia sempervirens (coast redwood), Sequoiadendron giganteum (giant sequoia). Together they have 49,610 occurrence records in GBIF; the best recorded is Sequoia sempervirens (coast redwood) with 28,316.

A family groups related genera. Species in the same family share a recent common ancestor and usually obvious features, which is why they are often arranged together in field guides.

Reading the numbers for this family

The 2 species belong to 2 genera: Sequoia (1), Sequoiadendron (1). GBIF holds 49,610 occurrence records for them together. The best recorded species, Sequoia sempervirens (coast redwood), has 57% of them and the least recorded, Sequoiadendron giganteum (giant sequoia), has 42.92%, so the record counts are very uneven within the family and say more about where people look than about the species themselves. 89% of the records that carry a year were made in the year 2000 or later, and 89% are human observations. Adding up the ten countries listed for each species, the countries with the most records are United States (59%), United Kingdom (20%), France (14%), Germany (2%). Record counts show where people made and published observations, so a country with many naturalists and good online systems appears often.

For 2 of the species the Backbone Taxonomy gives the place of first publication, which is shown on each species page. A first publication date or place tells you when and where the name was introduced and does not date the species.
