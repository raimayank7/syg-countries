Species, genus and family

A species is the basic unit of biological classification, usually described as a group of organisms that can breed with each other and produce fertile offspring. The definition has exceptions (for example in plants and microbes), but it works for most animals.

Species are grouped into genera and genera into families. Two species with the same genus, such as the lion Panthera leo and the tiger Panthera tigris, are close relatives; two in the same family but different genera, such as the lion and the cheetah Acinonyx jubatus (both Felidae), are more distant; two in the same order, such as the lion and the wolf (Carnivora), more distant still.
