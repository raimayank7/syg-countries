What is the NIC code?

The National Industrial Classification (NIC) is the standard list of economic activities used in India. It is published by the Central Statistical Organisation of the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation and is used by statistical surveys and by administrative registers to say what a business or establishment does.

History

According to the publication, the Central Statistical Organisation revised the Standard Industrial Classification of 1962 in 1970, again in 1987 and in 1998, and updated NIC-1998 to NIC-2004 in line with ISIC Rev. 3.1. The structure of NIC-2008 is identical to ISIC Rev. 4 up to the four-digit level (the class); classes were then divided into five-digit sub-classes according to national requirements.

How the code is built

  • Section: a letter, A to U (21 sections)
  • Division: 2 digits (88 divisions)
  • Group: 3 digits (238 groups)
  • Class: 4 digits (419 classes)
  • Sub-class: 5 digits (1,300 sub-classes)

For example, 01111 is "Growing of wheat": division 01 crop and animal production, group 011 growing of non-perennial crops, class 0111 growing of cereals, sub-class 01111.

Where the codes appear

Several government registrations and returns ask a business to state its activity by NIC code. Which digits are asked (two, four or five) and which version is expected depends on the form, so follow the form's own instructions. This site does not give advice on any registration.
