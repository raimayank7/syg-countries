WHOIS and RDAP: looking up a domain

When you want to know which registrar a domain is with, when it was registered or when it expires, you ask a registration data service. WHOIS is the old protocol; RDAP (Registration Data Access Protocol) is the newer, structured replacement defined by the IETF, which answers in a standard format over HTTPS.

How to ask

  • A web tool: many registrars and registries have a lookup form. For a country extension, the registry's own site is the most reliable. Every extension page on this site links to the registry.
  • WHOIS from a command line: whois example.com, or ask a particular server: whois -h whois.verisign-grs.com example.com. The page of each extension lists its WHOIS server.
  • RDAP in a browser: take the base address of the registry (listed on its page) and add domain/example.com. You get a structured document with dates, status codes and name servers.

What you will see

The answer usually shows the registrar, the creation, update and expiry dates, status codes such as "clientTransferProhibited", and the name servers. For many extensions the owner's name, address, email and phone are redacted or replaced by a privacy service, because of privacy law and registry policy. If you need to reach the owner, registries and registrars provide a contact form or an abuse address.

Status codes

Status codes tell what is allowed with the name. "ok" means nothing special. Those that start with "client" are set by the registrar (for example, to stop a transfer without the owner's permission). Those that start with "server" are set by the registry. "pendingDelete" means the name is about to be released.

How current is it

The data of each registry is separate; a lookup shows what that registry says now, not what this site says. The servers on this site's pages are those that IANA listed in November 2026.
