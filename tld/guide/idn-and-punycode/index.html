IDN and punycode explained

The Domain Name System was designed for the letters A to Z, digits and hyphen. To allow names in other scripts, an international standard (IDNA) converts every non-Latin name into an ASCII form before it is used in the DNS. The result starts with xn-- and is called punycode.

How it looks

These are real extensions from the root zone, with their ASCII forms:

Typed asUsed in the DNS asScript
.ελxn--qxamGreek
.ευxn--qxa6aGreek
.бгxn--90aeCyrillic
.белxn--90aisCyrillic
.еюxn--e1a4cCyrillic
.мкдxn--d1alfCyrillic

Your browser does the conversion for you. You type or see the native form, and the browser sends the xn-- form. In the address bar a browser may show the xn-- form instead when it thinks the name could be confused with another, for safety.

Why it matters

  • A person who reads and writes only Hindi, Arabic, Chinese or Russian can type an entire address in their own script.
  • Email addresses with a native extension need an email program that understands them; older systems do not.
  • Look-alike letters from different scripts can be used to imitate a known name, so be careful with links in messages.

How many exist

The root zone has 151 active non-Latin extensions, covering 23 scripts. See the full list by script on the non-Latin extensions page.
