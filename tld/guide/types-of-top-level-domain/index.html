Types of top-level domain

IANA has long sorted TLDs by who they are for and under what rules they were created. The root zone database now uses a short list of kinds, summarised here with today's counts.

Country-code TLDs (309 active)

These stand for a country or territory, such as .in, .uk or .de. Most have two letters taken from the ISO 3166 list. Each registry sets its own rules; some are open worldwide, some require a local presence. They are a clear country signal for search engines. There are also country-code TLDs in non-Latin scripts, which are listed on the internationalised extensions page.

Generic TLDs (1126 active)

These are not tied to a country. They include the oldest generic names (.com, .net, .org, .info and a few more), those once called "sponsored" because a community decides who may register (such as .museum or .aero), and the large group that began in 2012: words for topics, places, professions and brands. In the root zone database all of these are one kind, "generic", and the registry decides the rules. A brand registry (a company's own extension) does not sell names to the public.

Special-use and infrastructure

.arpa is the TLD for Internet infrastructure, such as turning an IP address back into a name. It is not for websites. A few other names are reserved for special uses, such as examples and local networks.

Test TLDs (retired)

In 2007 IANA delegated a set of internationalised test TLDs to try out non-Latin addresses. They have been retired.
