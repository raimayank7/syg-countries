How to choose a domain extension

The extension is the last thing people see in a web address and the first thing you must decide. There is no magic answer: it depends on whom you want to reach and on what you are allowed to register.

1. Whom do you serve?

If your customers are in one country, that country's extension (.in, .uk, .de, .au) tells people and search engines where you are. Google says that country-code extensions are tied to a specific country and therefore give a strong signal that a site is meant for it, and that it treats some vanity country extensions (such as .tv and .me) as generic. If you serve people in many countries, or want a name that is not about a place, a generic extension suits better.

2. Are you allowed to register it?

Registries set their own rules. Some country extensions accept anyone; some need a company or an address in the country; some reserve second levels such as .co or .com for businesses. Open the page of the extension you like on this site and follow the registry website link to read the eligibility rules, before you set up an email address on a name you may lose.

3. Is the registry sound?

Each page shows whether the extension is signed with DNSSEC, how many name servers it has and whether they work over IPv6, and its registry's website. A registry that offers WHOIS and RDAP lookups and has a clear website is easier to deal with. Among the 1436 active extensions, 1350 are signed with DNSSEC.

4. What will it cost to keep?

The registration price is only the first year. Renewal prices may be much higher, and prices differ a lot between extensions. Prices are not part of open data, so compare two or three registrars and read the renewal price, not only the first-year offer.

5. Will people recognise it?

Visitors recognise .com and their own country's extension easily. Newer extensions can be perfectly good but look unfamiliar, and spam filters may be stricter with some of them. If you can get the name you want under .com or your country's extension, that is usually the safe choice, and you can buy others to protect the name.

6. Read the whole address aloud

Read the name with its extension: some combinations read as unintended words. Keep it short, avoid hyphens and numbers where you can, and check that the same name is free as a social media account.
