Thymine (C₅H₆N₂O₂)

Molar mass 126.11 g/mol. PubChem CID 1135.

Thymine has the molecular formula C₅H₆N₂O₂ and a molar mass of 126.11 g/mol. In PubChem it is compound number 1135. Its systematic (IUPAC) name is 5-methyl-1H-pyrimidine-2,4-dione.

What Thymine is made of

One formula unit has 15 atoms of 4 elements. By mass, carbon makes up the largest share: 47.6 per cent. The molar mass is the sum of the atomic weights of all atoms: 5 x 12.011 + 2 x 15.999 + 2 x 14.007 + 6 x 1.008 = 126.11 g/mol.

Grams and moles of Thymine

One mole of Thymine weighs 126.11 g. So 10 g is 0.079 mol, and 100 g is 0.793 mol. A solution of 1 mol per litre contains 126.11 g of it in each litre. A mole is 6.02214076 x 10^23 particles (the Avogadro constant). The converter works both ways.

Hazard labelling

PubChem shows no harmonised EU classification (Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008) for Thymine, so none is given here. That is not a statement that the substance is harmless. For handling and safety, use the safety data sheet of the product you have.
