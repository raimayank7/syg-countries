Lithium Carbonate (CLi₂O₃)

Molar mass 73.90 g/mol. PubChem CID 11125.

Lithium Carbonate has the molecular formula CLi₂O₃ and a molar mass of 73.90 g/mol. In PubChem it is compound number 11125. Its systematic (IUPAC) name is dilithium;carbonate.

What Lithium Carbonate is made of

One formula unit has 6 atoms of 3 elements. By mass, oxygen makes up the largest share: 64.9 per cent. The molar mass is the sum of the atomic weights of all atoms: 3 x 15.999 + 2 x 7.000 + 12.011 = 74.01 g/mol.

PubChem lists 73.90 g/mol; the small difference from the sum above comes from rounding in PubChem or a different atomic-weight value for one element (for example lithium, whose weight varies with the source). This page uses the PubChem figure for the converter.

Grams and moles of Lithium Carbonate

One mole of Lithium Carbonate weighs 73.90 g. So 10 g is 0.135 mol, and 100 g is 1.353 mol. A solution of 1 mol per litre contains 73.90 g of it in each litre. A mole is 6.02214076 x 10^23 particles (the Avogadro constant). The converter works both ways.

Hazard labelling

PubChem shows no harmonised EU classification (Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008) for Lithium Carbonate, so none is given here. That is not a statement that the substance is harmless. For handling and safety, use the safety data sheet of the product you have.
