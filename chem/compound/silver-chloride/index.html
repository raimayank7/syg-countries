Silver Chloride (AgCl)

Molar mass 143.32 g/mol. PubChem CID 24561.

Silver Chloride has the molecular formula AgCl and a molar mass of 143.32 g/mol. In PubChem it is compound number 24561. Its systematic (IUPAC) name is chlorosilver.

What Silver Chloride is made of

One formula unit has 2 atoms of 2 elements. By mass, silver makes up the largest share: 75.3 per cent. The molar mass is the sum of the atomic weights of all atoms: 107.868 + 35.450 = 143.32 g/mol.

Grams and moles of Silver Chloride

One mole of Silver Chloride weighs 143.32 g. So 10 g is 0.070 mol, and 100 g is 0.698 mol. A solution of 1 mol per litre contains 143.32 g of it in each litre. A mole is 6.02214076 x 10^23 particles (the Avogadro constant). The converter works both ways.

Hazard labelling

PubChem shows no harmonised EU classification (Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008) for Silver Chloride, so none is given here. That is not a statement that the substance is harmless. For handling and safety, use the safety data sheet of the product you have.
