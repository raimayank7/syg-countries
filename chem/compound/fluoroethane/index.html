Fluoroethane (C₂H₅F)

Molar mass 48.06 g/mol. PubChem CID 9620.

Fluoroethane has the molecular formula C₂H₅F and a molar mass of 48.06 g/mol. In PubChem it is compound number 9620.

What Fluoroethane is made of

One formula unit has 8 atoms of 3 elements. By mass, carbon makes up the largest share: 50.0 per cent. The molar mass is the sum of the atomic weights of all atoms: 2 x 12.011 + 18.998 + 5 x 1.008 = 48.06 g/mol.

Grams and moles of Fluoroethane

One mole of Fluoroethane weighs 48.06 g. So 10 g is 0.208 mol, and 100 g is 2.081 mol. A solution of 1 mol per litre contains 48.06 g of it in each litre. A mole is 6.02214076 x 10^23 particles (the Avogadro constant). The converter works both ways.

Hazard labelling

PubChem shows no harmonised EU classification (Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008) for Fluoroethane, so none is given here. That is not a statement that the substance is harmless. For handling and safety, use the safety data sheet of the product you have.
