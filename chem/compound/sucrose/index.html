Sucrose (C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁)

Molar mass 342.30 g/mol. PubChem CID 5988.

Sucrose has the molecular formula C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ and a molar mass of 342.30 g/mol. In PubChem it is compound number 5988. Its systematic (IUPAC) name is (2R,3R,4S,5S,6R)-2-[(2S,3S,4S,5R)-3,4-dihydroxy-2,5-bis(hydroxymethyl)oxolan-2-yl]oxy-6-(hydroxymethyl)oxane-3,4,5-triol.

What Sucrose is made of

One formula unit has 45 atoms of 3 elements. By mass, oxygen makes up the largest share: 51.4 per cent. The molar mass is the sum of the atomic weights of all atoms: 11 x 15.999 + 12 x 12.011 + 22 x 1.008 = 342.30 g/mol.

Grams and moles of Sucrose

One mole of Sucrose weighs 342.30 g. So 10 g is 0.029 mol, and 100 g is 0.292 mol. A solution of 1 mol per litre contains 342.30 g of it in each litre. A mole is 6.02214076 x 10^23 particles (the Avogadro constant). The converter works both ways.

Hazard labelling

PubChem shows no harmonised EU classification (Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008) for Sucrose, so none is given here. That is not a statement that the substance is harmless. For handling and safety, use the safety data sheet of the product you have.
