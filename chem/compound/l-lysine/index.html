L-Lysine (C₆H₁₄N₂O₂)

Molar mass 146.19 g/mol. PubChem CID 5962.

L-Lysine has the molecular formula C₆H₁₄N₂O₂ and a molar mass of 146.19 g/mol. In PubChem it is compound number 5962. Its systematic (IUPAC) name is (2S)-2,6-diaminohexanoic acid.

What L-Lysine is made of

One formula unit has 24 atoms of 4 elements. By mass, carbon makes up the largest share: 49.3 per cent. The molar mass is the sum of the atomic weights of all atoms: 6 x 12.011 + 2 x 15.999 + 2 x 14.007 + 14 x 1.008 = 146.19 g/mol.

Grams and moles of L-Lysine

One mole of L-Lysine weighs 146.19 g. So 10 g is 0.068 mol, and 100 g is 0.684 mol. A solution of 1 mol per litre contains 146.19 g of it in each litre. A mole is 6.02214076 x 10^23 particles (the Avogadro constant). The converter works both ways.

Hazard labelling

PubChem shows no harmonised EU classification (Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008) for L-Lysine, so none is given here. That is not a statement that the substance is harmless. For handling and safety, use the safety data sheet of the product you have.
