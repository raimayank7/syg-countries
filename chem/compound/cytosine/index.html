Cytosine (C₄H₅N₃O)

Molar mass 111.10 g/mol. PubChem CID 597.

Cytosine has the molecular formula C₄H₅N₃O and a molar mass of 111.10 g/mol. In PubChem it is compound number 597. Its systematic (IUPAC) name is 6-amino-1H-pyrimidin-2-one.

What Cytosine is made of

One formula unit has 13 atoms of 4 elements. By mass, carbon makes up the largest share: 43.2 per cent. The molar mass is the sum of the atomic weights of all atoms: 4 x 12.011 + 3 x 14.007 + 15.999 + 5 x 1.008 = 111.10 g/mol.

Grams and moles of Cytosine

One mole of Cytosine weighs 111.10 g. So 10 g is 0.090 mol, and 100 g is 0.900 mol. A solution of 1 mol per litre contains 111.10 g of it in each litre. A mole is 6.02214076 x 10^23 particles (the Avogadro constant). The converter works both ways.

Hazard labelling

PubChem shows no harmonised EU classification (Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008) for Cytosine, so none is given here. That is not a statement that the substance is harmless. For handling and safety, use the safety data sheet of the product you have.
