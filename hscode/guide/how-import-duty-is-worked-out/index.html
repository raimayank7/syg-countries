How import duty is worked out

The rate in the tariff is a formula. The duty on a shipment is that formula applied to its value or its quantity, and it is only one part of the cost of importing.

Kinds of rate

  • Free: no duty. In the US tariff, 4,315 of the 11,414 lines that carry a general rate are free.
  • Ad valorem, a percentage of the customs value: 5% on goods worth 10,000 dollars is 500 dollars. 5,863 lines have a percentage rate, from 0.1% to 350%.
  • Specific, an amount per unit: for example 1 cent per kilogram on 5,000 kg is 50 dollars.
  • Compound: both, such as 3 cents per kilogram plus 5%.

The columns of the US tariff

"General" is the normal rate for countries with normal trade relations. "Special" lists lower or free rates for trade agreements and preference programmes, with letter codes for each programme. "Column 2" is the rate for the few countries without normal trade relations. The tables on this site show the general and column 2 rates.

What is not in the rate

Duties imposed under other laws or trade actions can apply on top of the tariff rate, and goods may also carry fees, taxes, quotas and licence rules. The tariff's own notes and the customs authority list the current ones. Always check the current rules before you quote a landed cost.
